# The Sighting Board — Content v1 (MA.3.8B-XP)

**Grade 3 · Math · Solara.** Written Sept 27, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/sighting-board.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.8B · **Also shows up under:** 3.8A

**I can:** I can read frequency tables, dot plots, picture graphs, and bar graphs with scales. I can use them to solve one-step and two-step problems.

**Mission:** Graph the animal sightings on the canopy tour and use the graphs to plan the tour.

**Story:** Solara's jungle moon is full of animals. The crew is running a canopy tour, and every sighting goes up on the board as a graph.

**Meters on Solara:** Tour ready (starts at 20%) · Tour stops · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. It's animal season on Solara's jungle moon. Our canopy tour spots toucans, sloths, monkeys, and frogs every day. Every sighting goes up on the Sighting Board as a graph. Help Kai and Nova read the graphs and plan the best tour yet. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Howler monkeys | Howler monkeys are some of the loudest land animals on Earth. Their calls can be heard about 3 miles away. |
| 2 | Blue morpho butterflies | A blue morpho's wings are bright blue on top and brown underneath. When it closes its wings, it can seem to disappear. |
| 3 | Glass frogs | Glass frogs have see-through skin on their bellies. You can look right in and see their hearts beating. |
| 5 | Macaw clay licks | Big flocks of macaws gather on riverbanks to eat clay. Scientists think the clay gives them salt they can't get from fruit. |
| 6 | Kinkajous | Kinkajous come out at night. They use their long tongues to lick nectar from flowers, so some people call them honey bears. |
| 8 | Frog piggyback rides | Some poison dart frog parents carry their tadpoles on their backs. They climb up trees to drop them into tiny pools of water inside plants. |
| 10 | Spider monkeys | Spider monkeys use their long tails like an extra hand. They can hang from a branch by just their tail. |
| 11 | Giant river otters | Giant river otters can grow almost as long as a grown-up is tall. They live and hunt fish in family groups. |
| 13 | Harpy eagles | Harpy eagles have back claws about as long as a grizzly bear's. They swoop through the canopy to catch sloths and monkeys. |
| 15 | Red-eyed tree frogs | Red-eyed tree frogs sleep on leaves in the day. If something scares them, they pop open their big red eyes to surprise it. |

# The Water Cycle Loop — Content v1 (SCI.4.10A-XP)

**Grade 4 · Science · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 26, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/water-cycle-loop.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.10A · **Also shows up under:** 4.1B, 4.1D, 4.1G, 4.2B, 4.3A, 4.3C, 4.5B, 4.5E

**I can:** I can describe and draw how water moves through the water cycle, above and on Earth's surface. I can explain how the Sun's energy makes the water cycle go.

**Mission:** Follow Drop 7 from the ocean to the clouds and back, and prove that the water cycle is a loop powered by the Sun.

**Story:** The sky reef of Cloudreach needs new rain pools, and the crew has to prove where the rain comes from. They tag one water drop and follow it all the way around.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Rain flow (starts at 30%) · Drop logs · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

So Kai tagged one water drop with a tiny tracker. We'll call it Drop 7. Water here works just like it does on Earth. Follow the drop. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Sun power | The Sun is the main source of energy for the water cycle. Sunlight warms oceans, lakes, and soil so water can evaporate. |
| 4 | Rain gauges | A rain gauge is a tool that collects rain so scientists can measure how much fell. Rainfall is often measured in millimeters or inches. |
| 5 | Water vapor | Water vapor is water as an invisible gas. The air always holds some water vapor, even on a sunny day. |
| 6 | Very old water | Earth's water has been cycling for billions of years. The water in your glass may once have fallen as rain on dinosaurs. |
| 8 | Transpiration | Plants pull water up from their roots and give off water vapor through tiny holes in their leaves. This is called transpiration. |
| 9 | The salty ocean | About 97% of Earth's water is salt water in the ocean. Only a small part is fresh water in ice, lakes, rivers, and underground. |
| 10 | Ocean vapor | Most of the water vapor in Earth's air evaporated from the oceans, because the oceans are so big and the Sun warms them every day. |
| 11 | Groundwater | Groundwater fills tiny spaces between grains of sand and soil and cracks in rock. Many people get drinking water from wells that reach groundwater. |
| 13 | Tides | The Moon's gravity pulls on the oceans and causes tides. It doesn't make water evaporate. The Sun's heat does that. |
| 15 | Mountain snow | In many places, snow that melts on mountains in spring fills rivers and lakes and gives people fresh water. |

# The Plant Survivors — Content v1 (SCI.4.13A-XP)

**Grade 4 · Science · Lumara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/plant-survivors.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.13A · **Also shows up under:** 4.13B, 4.1A, 4.1B, 4.1G, 4.2B, 4.3A, 4.3B, 4.3C, 4.5B, 4.5D

**I can:** I can explain how plant structures, like waxy leaves and deep roots, help plants survive where they live. I can tell inherited traits from acquired traits.

**Mission:** Match real Earth plants to their homes, test how their parts work, and design a brand-new plant survivor.

**Story:** A seed ship from Earth has landed on Lumara, full of tough plants from deserts, tundra, rainforests, ponds, and windy coasts. The crew has to figure out how each one survives so it can grow in the right Wild Zone dome.

**Meters on Lumara:** Wild Zone (starts at 30%) · Survivor cards · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. A seed ship from Earth just landed in Lumara's glowing garden. It's full of tough plants from deserts, frozen tundra, rainforests, ponds, and windy coasts. We're building a Wild Zone with a dome for each place. Each plant has special parts that help it survive. Figure out how they work, and we'll plant every one in the right home. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Mesquite roots | Mesquite trees grow in dry parts of Texas. Scientists once found a mesquite root about 50 meters underground. That's deeper than a 15-story building is tall! |
| 3 | Spines are leaves | A cactus spine is really a leaf that changed shape over a very long time. Thin spines lose much less water than flat leaves, and they also shade the stem a little. |
| 5 | Sun-tracking poppies | Arctic poppy flowers turn to face the Sun as it moves. Their cup shape bounces sunlight to the middle, so the center of the flower stays warmer than the air. Insects like to rest there. |
| 7 | Wax from palm leaves | In dry parts of Brazil, the carnauba palm coats its leaves in thick wax to keep water in. People scrape off the wax and use it to make shoe polish, car wax, and shiny candy coatings. |
| 8 | Wind-shaped trees | On windy coasts and mountains, some trees grow bent, with branches only on one side, pointing away from the wind. They are called flag trees. Their seeds grow straight if they sprout in a calm place. |
| 9 | Giant water lilies | Giant water lilies from the Amazon grow round leaves up to about 3 meters across. Strong ribs underneath hold air and spread out the weight, and sharp spines on the bottom keep fish from nibbling. |
| 10 | Drip tips | Many rainforest leaves end in a long, pointed drip tip. Rain runs off the tip in seconds, so tiny plants like moss and mold have a harder time growing on the leaf and blocking its light. |
| 11 | The Texas state plant | The prickly pear cactus is the state plant of Texas. Its flat pads are really stems. People cook the young pads and eat the red or purple fruit, called tunas, after carefully taking off the spines. |
| 12 | Sea oats | Sea oats grow on sand dunes along the Texas Gulf Coast. Their long roots grab the sand, and when sand piles up on them, they keep growing up through it. This helps build and hold the dunes. |
| 13 | Resurrection ferns | Resurrection ferns grow on live oak branches in Texas. In a drought they curl up and turn brown, but they don't die. After a rain, they can turn green and open up again in about a day. |

**I can:** I can graph ordered pairs in the first quadrant, including points from a number pattern or an input-output table. I can describe how to plot a point.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Weather radar | Weather radar sends out radio waves. The waves bounce off raindrops, hail, and snow and come back, so forecasters can see where it is raining. |
| 2 | Weather balloons | Every day, weather balloons carry small instruments called radiosondes high into the sky. They measure temperature, wind, and how wet the air is. |
| 3 | The eye of a storm | The calm center of a hurricane is called the eye. The strongest winds swirl in a ring around it called the eyewall. |
| 4 | Map grids | Maps of Earth use a grid of lines called latitude and longitude. A pair of numbers can name any place on Earth, a lot like an ordered pair. |
| 5 | Thunder | Light travels much faster than sound. That's why you see a flash of lightning before you hear the thunder. |
| 8 | Thunderstorm clouds | Thunderstorm clouds are called cumulonimbus clouds. They can tower higher than Mount Everest, the tallest mountain on Earth. |
| 9 | Hail | Hailstones grow when strong winds toss them up and down inside a storm cloud. Each trip adds another layer of ice. |
| 10 | Tracking hurricanes | Forecasters plot a hurricane's position on a map every few hours. Connecting the points shows its path and helps them predict where it will go. |
| 14 | Lightning | Lightning flashes somewhere on Earth more than 40 times every second. |
| 15 | Rainbows | A rainbow forms when sunlight shines through raindrops. The drops bend the light and split it into its colors. |

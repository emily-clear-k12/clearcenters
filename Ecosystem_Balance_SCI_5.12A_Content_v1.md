# The Ecosystem Balance — Content v1 (SCI.5.12A-XP)

**Grade 5 · Science · Solara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/ecosystem-balance.js`.

**Main standard:** 5.12A · **Also shows up under:** 5.12B, 5.1A, 5.2B, 5.2D, 5.3A, 5.3B, 5.3C, 5.5E, 5.5F, 5.5G

**I can:** I can describe how organisms survive by interacting with living and nonliving parts of an ecosystem. I can trace energy in a food web and predict what happens when something in the ecosystem changes.

**Mission:** Sort the living and nonliving parts of the forest, trace energy through the food web, and predict how changes will ripple through the ecosystem.

**Story:** The river research station on Solara watches over a real rainforest ecosystem. A storm, a missing predator, and a long dry season are putting its balance to the test.

**Meters on Solara:** Balance (starts at 25%) · Web links · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. We're at the river research station on Solara. This jungle works just like a rainforest on Earth. Every living thing depends on other living things and on sunlight, water, air, and soil. Something is throwing the forest out of balance. Help us figure out what. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Root helpers | Many fungi grow on and around tree roots. They help the tree soak up water and nutrients, and the tree shares some of its sugar with them. |
| 4 | Air plants | Plants that grow on other plants are called epiphytes. Many orchids and ferns are epiphytes. They get water from rain and damp air instead of from soil. |
| 5 | Fast decomposers | In a warm, wet rainforest, a fallen leaf can break down in just a few months. In a cold forest, the same leaf can take years. |
| 6 | Thin soil | Rainforest soil is thin and has few nutrients. Most of the nutrients are stored in living plants, and decomposers quickly recycle them when things die. |
| 7 | Making food from light | Plants use energy from sunlight to turn water and carbon dioxide from the air into sugar. This is called photosynthesis, and it gives off the oxygen we breathe. |
| 10 | Top predators | Jaguars are top predators. Adult jaguars have almost no natural predators. By hunting plant eaters, they help keep plants from being eaten up. |
| 11 | Forgetful gardeners | Agoutis are one of the few animals with teeth strong enough to open a Brazil nut shell. The nuts they bury and forget grow into new trees. |
| 12 | Termite recyclers | Termites eat dead wood and leaves. Tiny living things in their guts help them digest the wood, and this returns nutrients to the forest. |
| 13 | Energy steps | When an animal eats, only about one tenth of the energy in its food gets passed on to the animal that eats it. Most is used to move, grow, and stay warm. |
| 15 | Forests make rain | Rainforest trees give off huge amounts of water vapor through their leaves. That vapor helps form clouds, so a big rainforest makes much of its own rain. |

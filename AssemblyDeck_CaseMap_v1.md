# Assembly Deck — Case Map v1 (48 new cases)

**Sept 22, 2026. Draft for Emily's review before authoring.** Four new cases per subject per grade, on top of the six Wave 1 cases already live. Every case names a standard checked in this session against Emily's own PDFs — Grade 3/4/5 Science (adopted 2021, implemented 2024–25), Grade 3/4/5 Social Studies (adopted 2022, implemented 2024–25), ELAR Grades 3–5, and Mathematics Grades 3–5. No code is reused from the six existing cases.

**Codes:** `<standard>-AD`, with the `SS.` / `ELA.` / `MA.` prefix so a Science 4.6A can never collide with a Math 4.6A. Each case is three rounds plus the assembly round, ~20 minutes, and ships with the Wave 1 extras (protest lines, a requester who writes back, an Editor's Trap, Chief's Challenge).

**Math needs no new engine.** The word-problem mode is the same slots-and-tray machinery with different round shapes:

1. **Build the problem** — Situation · The numbers (2) · The question. Decoys: a number the question doesn't need, a question the numbers can't answer, a fact from the wrong story.
2. **Build the representation** — The equation · What the letter stands for · The matching diagram. Decoys: the right numbers with the wrong operation, an equation that answers a different question, a diagram with the parts reversed.
3. **Solve and check** — Step one · Step two · The answer · The check. Decoys: the first step presented as the answer, an answer with no unit or label, a "check" that just restates the answer instead of testing it.

The only client addition is an ungraded scratch pad on Math cases.

**Reading level.** Every case is gated to its grade band before it ships — grade 3 at FK 2.0–4.2 with sentences averaging 11 words or less, grade 4 at 3.5–5.6 / 14 words, grade 5 at 5.0–7.2 / 16 words, with a floor as well as a ceiling and a required gap between grades. The Wave 1 six were rewritten to meet it. Target vocabulary is exempt: the sentence around a word like *deposition* carries the load instead.

# Science

## Grade 3
- **`3.12B-AD`** · TEKS 3.12B *Food Chains* — **The Pond That Went Quiet.** A pond's minnows vanish and the class has to explain the chain, not guess at a villain. Paragraphs: where the energy starts · what eats what · what happens upstream of a break. Decoys: a favorite-animal opinion, a predator blamed with no evidence, a food *web* fact that belongs to a different grade's lesson.
- **`3.11B-AD`** · TEKS 3.11B *Conservation of Natural Resources* — **The Water Bill.** The school's water use jumped and the custodian wants the facts before the principal does. Paragraphs: what the meter shows · where it's going · what to change first. Decoys: a guilt-trip opinion, a claim the meter contradicts, an unrelated recycling fact.
- **`3.13A-AD`** · TEKS 3.13A *Animal Structures & Survival* — **Built for This.** Three animals, three structures, one question about what each one is *for*. Paragraphs: the structure · what it does · why it fits that habitat. Decoys: "cutest animal" opinion, a behavior mistaken for a structure, a made-up adaptation.
- **`3.10C-AD`** · TEKS 3.10C *Rapid Changes to Earth's Surface* — **The Road That Moved.** A hillside slid over a road overnight. Paragraphs: what changed · how fast · slow changes that were happening too. Decoys: an alarmist opinion, a claim the photos contradict, a weather fact with no link to the slide.

## Grade 4
- **`4.8B-AD`** · TEKS 4.8B *Conductors & Insulators* — **The Cold Lunchbox Job.** Testing which materials keep lunch cold. Paragraphs: what was tested · what the data shows · which material to buy. Decoys: a brand-name opinion, a temperature claim the data contradicts, a magnetism fact from the wrong test.
- **`4.9B-AD`** · TEKS 4.9B *Moon Patterns* — **Twenty-Eight Nights.** A month of moon sketches, one pattern. Paragraphs: what was recorded · the pattern · what it predicts for next week. Decoys: a superstition stated as fact, a claim the sketches contradict, a fact about eclipses that isn't part of this pattern.
- **`4.12B-AD`** · TEKS 4.12B *Food Webs* — **When the Owls Left.** One species leaves a meadow web. Paragraphs: the web before · what shifted · what the numbers do next. Decoys: an opinion about "useless" animals, a reversed arrow claim, a fact about a different ecosystem.
- **`4.11B-AD`** · TEKS 4.11B *Energy Resources & Environmental Impact* — **Two Ways to Power the School.** Solar panels or the existing grid hookup. Paragraphs: option one · option two · the honest trade-off. Decoys: a slogan, a cost claim the table contradicts, an unsupported claim about jobs.

## Grade 5
- **`5.6B-AD`** · TEKS 5.6B *Mixtures* — **The Sorting Table.** Separating a mixture without changing what's in it. Paragraphs: what's in the mixture · how each part was separated · what that proves about mixtures. Decoys: an opinion about the "best" method, a claim that a property changed, a solution fact that belongs to 5.6C.
- **`5.8C-AD`** · TEKS 5.8C *Light* — **The Bent Straw.** Reflection and refraction at one kitchen table. Paragraphs: what the mirror did · what the water did · why the straw looks broken. Decoys: a "magic" explanation, a claim the demo contradicts, an unrelated fact about color.
- **`5.12B-AD`** · TEKS 5.12B *Changes in Food Webs* — **The Bay After the Storm.** A hurricane reshapes a bay's web. Paragraphs: what the storm changed · what the counts show · what recovery depends on. Decoys: a blame-the-weather opinion, a count the data contradicts, an unsupported prediction about next year.
- **`5.10C-AD`** · TEKS 5.10C *Landform Formation* — **Reading a Canyon.** Layers, a river, and a very long time. Paragraphs: what the layers show · what the river is doing now · what that means for the rate of change. Decoys: an awe-struck opinion, a claim the rock record contradicts, a fact about volcanoes that isn't this canyon.

# Social Studies

## Grade 3
- **`SS.3.5B-AD`** · TEKS 3.5B *Creating a Budget* — **The Field Trip Fund.** A class budget that has to balance. Paragraphs: what's coming in · what's going out · what to cut first. Decoys: a wish stated as a need, a total the ledger contradicts, an off-topic fundraiser story.
- **`SS.3.7C-AD`** · TEKS 3.7C *Government Services* — **Who Do You Call?** Three problems, three levels of government. Paragraphs: the local job · the state job · the national job. Decoys: an opinion about taxes, a service assigned to the wrong level, an unrelated fact about elections.
- **`SS.3.9A-AD`** · TEKS 3.9A *Characteristics of Good Citizenship* — **The Playground Rule.** Citizenship as something people do, not feel. Paragraphs: what happened · what good citizenship looked like here · what it would look like next time. Decoys: a "nice people" opinion, a claim the account contradicts, a rule fact that isn't citizenship.
- **`SS.3.14B-AD`** · TEKS 3.14B *Primary & Secondary Sources* — **Two Accounts of the Same Day.** A diary page and a newspaper article disagree. Paragraphs: what the primary source says · what the secondary source says · how to weigh them. Decoys: "older means truer" opinion, a claim neither source makes, a fact about a different event.

- **`SS.4.3D-AD`** · TEKS 4.3D *Republic of Texas* — **A Country With No Money.** The Republic's successes and problems side by side. Paragraphs: what worked · what didn't · why annexation kept coming up. Decoys: a hero-worship opinion, a claim the record contradicts, a fact from after statehood.
- **`SS.4.4B-AD`** · TEKS 4.4B *Texas Cattle Industry* — **The Price of a Longhorn.** Cattle drives as an economic answer. Paragraphs: why prices differed · how the drives worked · what ended them. - **`SS.4.11C-AD`** · TEKS 4.11C *Factors Affecting Texas Economic Growth* — **What Built This Town.** Four factors, one town's growth. Paragraphs: the geography · the people who came · the industry that followed. Decoys: a boosterish opinion, a claim the census contradicts, a fact about a different town.

## Grade 5
- **`SS.5.2A-AD`** · TEKS 5.2A *Causes & Effects of the American Revolution* — **Before the Shooting.** Causes that built up over a decade. Paragraphs: the taxes · the responses · what made it a war. Decoys: a patriotic slogan, a claim the timeline contradicts, an effect listed as a cause.
- **`SS.5.14A-AD`** · TEKS 5.14A *Declaration of Independence* — **What the Document Actually Says.** Purpose, key elements, importance. - **`SS.5.15B-AD`** · TEKS 5.15B *Checks & Balances* — **The Bill That Didn't Pass.** One bill, three branches. Paragraphs: what each branch did · where it stopped · why that's the design working. - **`SS.5.12B-AD`** · TEKS 5.12B *Geography & Economic Activity* — **Why the Factory Is There.** Geography behind an industry's location. Paragraphs: the resource · the transport · the workforce. Decoys: a luck-based opinion, a claim the map contradicts, an unsupported claim about wages.

# ELAR

## Grade 3
- **`ELA.3.12A-AD`** · TEKS 3.12A *compose literary texts, including personal narratives* — **The Day the Bus Was Late.** Building a narrative that actually moves. Paragraphs: the setup · the problem · how it ended and what changed. Decoys: an explanation that kills the scene, an event out of sequence, a detail from a different day.
- **`ELA.3.12C-AD`** · TEKS 3.12C *compose argumentative texts, including opinion essays* — **Keep the Library Open at Lunch.** An opinion with reasons, not volume. Paragraphs: the opinion · the reasons · what to do about the one real objection. Decoys: an insult, a made-up number, an off-topic complaint.
- **`ELA.3.12D-AD`** · TEKS 3.12D *compose correspondence such as thank you notes or letters* — **The Letter to the Fire Station.** Parts of a letter, in order and in voice. Paragraphs: greeting and why you're writing · the specific thanks · the closing. Decoys: a demand instead of a thank-you, a fact that didn't happen, an unrelated question.
- **`ELA.3.7D-AD`** · TEKS 3.7D *retell and paraphrase texts in ways that maintain meaning and logical order* — **Say It Shorter.** Summarizing a short article without losing it. Paragraphs: the main idea · the two details that carry it · the ending. Decoys: a copied sentence that's too long, a detail that changes the meaning, the student's own opinion about the article.

## Grade 4
- **`ELA.4.12B-AD`** · TEKS 4.12B *compose informational texts* — **How the Cafeteria Line Works.** Informational writing about something readers do daily. Paragraphs: the system · the steps · what makes it faster. Decoys: a complaint, an exaggeration, a story about one bad day.
- **`ELA.4.12C-AD`** · TEKS 4.12C *compose argumentative texts, including opinion essays* — **Later Recess.** Claim, evidence, counterargument. Paragraphs: the claim · the evidence · the objection answered. Decoys: name-calling, a statistic the survey contradicts, one friend's experience as proof.
- **`ELA.4.12D-AD`** · TEKS 4.12D *compose correspondence that requests information* — **The Letter to the Museum.** Asking well enough to get an answer. Paragraphs: who you are and why you're writing · the specific questions · the thank-you and how to reach you. Decoys: a vague question, a demand, a fact about your weekend.
- **`ELA.4.7D-AD`** · TEKS 4.7D *retell, paraphrase, or summarize texts* — **The Article and the Rumor.** Summary versus what people say the article said. Paragraphs: what it actually claims · the support it gives · what it never says. Decoys: a claim the article never made, a detail inflated, a reaction instead of a summary.

## Grade 5
- **`ELA.5.12B-AD`** · TEKS 5.12B *compose informational texts* — **What a Watershed Is.** Explaining a system clearly to someone who's never heard of it. Paragraphs: the definition · how it works · why it matters here. Decoys: a persuasive line, a claim the source contradicts, a detail that belongs in a different explanation.
- **`ELA.5.12D-AD`** · TEKS 5.12D *compose correspondence that requests information* — **The Request to the City Engineer.** A real request with real questions. Paragraphs: the context · the questions · the close. Decoys: a rant, a question already answered in the packet, an off-topic aside.
- **`ELA.5.7D-AD`** · TEKS 5.7D *retell, paraphrase, or summarize texts* — **Two Summaries, One Article.** One summary is honest, one is slanted. Paragraphs: the main claim · the evidence the author gives · what a fair summary leaves out. Decoys: a slanted paraphrase, a quote taken out of context, the summarizer's own opinion.
- **`ELA.5.13D-AD`** · TEKS 5.13D *understand credibility of primary and secondary sources* — **Four Sources, One Question.** Sorting sources by what they can actually tell you. Paragraphs: what makes a source credible here · what each source can answer · which one to lead with. Decoys: "it was online so it's wrong," a credential that doesn't apply to this question, a source about a different topic.

## Grade 3

- **`MA.3.5B-AD`** · TEKS 3.5B *represent and solve one- and two-step multiplication and division problems within 100 using arrays, strip diagrams, and equations* — **Chairs for the Assembly.** Rows, seats, and one leftover row.

- **`MA.3.8B-AD`** · TEKS 3.8B *solve one- and two-step problems using categorical data represented with a frequency table, dot plot, pictograph, or bar graph* — **The Pet Survey.** A graph that answers some questions and not others.

## Grade 4
- **`MA.4.5A-AD`** · TEKS 4.5A *represent multi-step problems involving the four operations with whole numbers using strip diagrams and equations with a letter standing for the unknown quantity* — **The Field Trip Buses.** Students, seats, and a letter for what you don't know yet.
- **`MA.4.4H-AD`** · TEKS 4.4H *solve with fluency one- and two-step problems involving multiplication and division, including interpreting remainders* — **What the Remainder Means.** The same remainder, three different real answers.
- **`MA.4.5B-AD`** · TEKS 4.5B *represent problems using an input-output table and numerical expressions to generate a number pattern* — **The Sticker Machine.** Finding the rule, then using it.

- **`MA.5.3L-AD`** · TEKS 5.3L *divide whole numbers by unit fractions and unit fractions by whole numbers* — **Cutting the Ribbon.** How many quarter-yard pieces fit, and why the answer is bigger than you expect.
- **`MA.5.9C-AD`** · TEKS 5.9C *solve one- and two-step problems using data from a frequency table, dot plot, bar graph, stem-and-leaf plot, or scatterplot* — **The Science Fair Scores.** Two graphs of the same data, one question.

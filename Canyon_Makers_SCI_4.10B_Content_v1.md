# The Canyon Makers — Content v1 (SCI.4.10B-XP)

**Grade 4 · Science · Cindara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/canyon-makers.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.10B · **Also shows up under:** 4.1B, 4.1E, 4.1G, 4.2B, 4.3A, 4.3C, 4.5A, 4.5B, 4.5G

**I can:** I can model and describe how weathering, erosion, and deposition from water, wind, and ice slowly change Earth's surface.

**Mission:** Use the time-lapse camera to find out how the river carved the canyon and built the new delta, so the crew can plan a safe bridge.

**Story:** A river is carving a canyon near the Cindara outpost. The crew fast-forwards it with a time-lapse camera to see how rock breaks, moves, and settles.

**Meters on Cindara:** Canyon mapped (starts at 25%) · Rock samples · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. A river near our Cindara outpost is carving a deep canyon. We want to build a bridge across it, so we need to know how it formed and how it's still changing. Kai set up a time-lapse camera that can fast-forward millions of years. Rock here breaks, moves, and settles just like it does on Earth. Watch closely. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Stone arches | Arches National Park in Utah has more than 2,000 natural stone arches. Weathering and erosion slowly shaped them over a very long time. |
| 2 | Root power | Tree roots can grow into tiny cracks in rock. As the roots get thicker, they slowly push the rock apart. That's why roots can crack sidewalks too. |
| 3 | Hoodoos | Bryce Canyon in Utah has thousands of tall, thin rock towers called hoodoos. Ice wedging helps shape them, because water freezes and thaws in their cracks on many nights each year. |
| 5 | The Grand Canyon | The Colorado River carved the Grand Canyon in Arizona over millions of years. In places, the canyon is more than 1.6 kilometers (1 mile) deep. |
| 7 | Deltas | A delta is land built from sand and mud a river drops where it meets a lake or ocean. Deltas are named after the Greek letter delta (Δ), because the Nile River's delta is shaped like a triangle. |
| 8 | Tall sand dunes | Great Sand Dunes National Park in Colorado has the tallest sand dunes in North America. The tallest is about 230 meters (750 feet) high. Wind piled up all that sand. |
| 10 | Santa Elena Canyon | In Big Bend National Park in Texas, the Rio Grande carved Santa Elena Canyon. Its rock walls rise about 460 meters (1,500 feet) above the river. |
| 11 | Moraines | A moraine is a ridge of rocks and sand left behind by a glacier. Much of Long Island in New York is made of moraines dropped by glaciers thousands of years ago. |
| 13 | Palo Duro Canyon | Palo Duro Canyon in the Texas Panhandle is the second-largest canyon in the United States. A branch of the Red River slowly carved it over a very long time. |
| 15 | Padre Island | Padre Island in Texas is one of the longest barrier islands in the world. It is made of sand that waves and currents dropped along the coast. |

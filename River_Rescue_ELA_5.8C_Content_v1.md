# The River Rescue — Content v1 (ELA.5.8C-XP)

**Grade 5 · ELAR · Solara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/river-rescue.js`.

**Main standard:** 5.8C · **Also shows up under:** 5.10B, 5.8B, 5.6F, 5.7C, 5.3B, 5.7D

**I can:** I can analyze a story's plot, including the rising action, climax, falling action, and resolution, and explain how an author's structure fits their purpose.

**Mission:** Pin every part of the plot, find the real turning point, tell the falling action from the resolution, and compare how two authors structure their stories.

**Story:** On the jungle moon Solara, two young river guides race a flash flood to rescue stranded travelers. Their story is told in five parts, and a second story jumps back in time.

**Meters on Solara:** River calm (starts at 15%) · Plot pins · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega at the river camp near Reedwater, on the jungle moon Solara. The rainy season is here. The village's river guides told us their favorite story, The River Rescue, about two young guides and a flash flood. They say every new guide has to understand how it's built. Help us pin every part of the plot. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The River Rescue, Part 1: Reedwater (Realistic fiction (exposition))

On the jungle moon Solara, the Tumbling River ran slow and brown past the village of Reedwater. `A1.1` Ama and her cousin Teo were the youngest river guides in the village. `A1.2` Every day, they paddled travelers up and down the river in a long wooden canoe called the Heron. `A1.3`

Teo was loud and brave, and he always sat in the back to steer. `A2.1` Ama was quiet, and she knew every rock and bend of the river by heart. `A2.2` But ever since she tipped the Heron over at Kettle Bend last rainy season, her hands shook whenever the water ran fast. `A2.3` She had not paddled through Kettle Bend since. `A2.4`

Grandmother Suli, the oldest guide in Reedwater, had taught Ama one important trick. `A3.1` "When rocks hide under fast water, look for a smooth, dark V pointing downstream," she said. `A3.2` "The V is the safe path between them." `A3.3`

One sunny morning, Ama and Teo took a mapmaker and her two sons to Stone Island, a flat rock in the middle of the river. `A4.1` The travelers wanted to sketch the rare blue orchids that grew there. `A4.2` "We'll pick you up before sunset," Teo promised. `A4.3`

### Passage B · The River Rescue, Part 2: The River Rises (Realistic fiction (rising action))

At noon, dark clouds piled up over the mountains far upriver. `B1.1` Not a drop of rain fell on Reedwater, but Ama watched the river closely. `B1.2` A branch floated past, and then a whole tree, roots and all. `B1.3`

"Flash flood," Ama whispered. `B2.1` Heavy rain in the mountains was rushing into the river all at once, faster than the ground could soak it up. `B2.2` Within minutes, the water rose past the dock posts and turned the color of chocolate milk. `B2.3` Then a floating tree slammed into the dock, and half of it broke away with a crack like thunder. `B2.4`

Across the river, the mapmaker and her sons waved their arms from Stone Island. `B3.1` The brown water was already swallowing the edges of the rock. `B3.2` The village's big rescue raft was far downstream, two hours away. `B3.3`

"We have to take the Heron," Teo said, grabbing a coil of rope. `B4.1` Ama's stomach twisted into a knot. `B4.2` The only way to reach Stone Island ran straight through Kettle Bend. `B4.3`

### Passage C · The River Rescue, Part 3: Kettle Bend (Realistic fiction (climax))

Teo pushed off, and the Heron bucked on the waves like a wild horse. `C1.1` Cold water splashed over the sides, and Ama bailed it out with a gourd as fast as she could. `C1.2`

At Kettle Bend, the river roared around a crowd of big black rocks. `C2.1` Suddenly, Teo's paddle struck a hidden log and snapped in two. `C2.2` The canoe spun sideways toward the rocks. `C2.3`

"Ama, I can't steer!" Teo shouted. `C3.1` Ama's hands froze on her paddle, just like last year. `C3.2` Then, between two rocks, she spotted a smooth, dark V pointing downstream. `C3.3`

Ama took a deep breath, scrambled to the back, and dug her paddle in hard. `C4.1` She steered the Heron straight down the V, and it shot through Kettle Bend without touching a single rock. `C4.2` On the other side, the river spread out wide and calm, and Stone Island was right in front of them. `C4.3`

### Passage D · The River Rescue, Part 4: The Way Back (Realistic fiction (falling action))

The mapmaker and her sons waded through knee-deep water and climbed into the Heron. `D1.1` Teo sat in the middle and paddled with the broken half of his paddle. `D1.2` "You steer," he told Ama. "You're better at it." `D1.3`

The trip back was slow, but Ama's hands did not shake anymore. `D2.1` She kept the canoe in the calm water along the edge of the river, where the current was weaker. `D2.2` Halfway home, they met the big rescue raft coming upriver, and its crew tossed them a rope. `D2.3`

The raft towed the Heron the rest of the way to Reedwater. `D3.1` Behind them, the last of Stone Island disappeared under the brown water. `D3.2` Grandmother Suli was waiting on what was left of the dock with warm blankets. `D3.3`

### Passage E · The River Rescue, Part 5: Slow and Brown Again (Realistic fiction (resolution))

Three days later, the Tumbling River ran slow and brown again. `E1.1` The mapmaker sent the village a painting of the Heron shooting through Kettle Bend, and Grandmother Suli hung it by the new dock. `E1.2`

Teo carved himself a new paddle. `E2.1` But from then on, whenever the Heron went through Kettle Bend, Ama sat in the back and steered. `E2.2` "The river didn't get any easier," she told the younger children who wanted to be guides. `E2.3` "I just learned how to read it." `E2.4`

### Passage F · The Signal Drum (Realistic fiction (with a flashback))

Every time the river flooded, Grandfather Obo carried the old signal drum down to the dock. `F1.1` He beat it slowly all night long: boom, boom, boom. `F1.2` His granddaughter Nia rolled her eyes. "Nobody is out on the river tonight," she said. `F1.3`

Obo stopped drumming and looked out into the fog. `F2.1` "Sixty years ago, somebody was," he said quietly. `F2.2`

Sixty years earlier, Obo was ten years old, and he had paddled out alone to check his family's fish traps. `F3.1` The river rose fast, and thick fog rolled in until he could not see the front of his own canoe. `F3.2` He drifted for hours, cold and scared, with no idea which way was home. `F3.3`

Then, through the fog, he heard a sound: boom, boom, boom. `F4.1` It was his father, beating the signal drum on the dock. `F4.2` Obo paddled toward the sound until his canoe bumped against the dock posts. `F4.3`

Back on the dock, Nia stared at the old drum for a long moment. `F5.1` Then she picked up the second drumstick and sat down beside her grandfather. `F5.2` Together, they drummed until morning. `F5.3`

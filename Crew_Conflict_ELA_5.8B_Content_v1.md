# The Crew Conflict — Content v1 (ELA.5.8B-XP)

**Grade 5 · ELAR · Cindara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/crew-conflict.js`.

**Main standard:** 5.8B · **Also shows up under:** 5.8D, 5.8C, 5.6F, 5.7C, 5.3B, 5.11D

**I can:** I can analyze the relationships and conflicts among characters and explain how a setting and a conflict change the characters.

**Mission:** Read their story, their field log, and a letter home. Sort the conflicts, trace how the relationships change, and write the analysis for Cinder Hollow's history book.

**Story:** On the lava world of Cindara, three young volcano scientists argue about whether to finish collecting lava samples or run as Mount Kettle rumbles.

**Meters on Cindara:** Conflict map (starts at 20%) · Character cards · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega at the Cinder Hollow Lookout on Cindara. A team of three young volcano scientists just had a close call on the Glass Fields. They made it home, but they almost didn't, because they couldn't agree on when to leave. Dr. Marlow, their mentor, wants to understand what happened between them. Help us read their story and figure out the conflict. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The Last Sample, Part 1 (Short story)

The Red Vent hissed like a kettle about to boil. `A1.1` Lina Sol knelt at its edge, scooping glowing lava into a steel sample cup. `A1.2` Her younger brother, Teo, held the tremor meter, and their friend Ash Kerr packed each cooled sample into the case. `A1.3` The three junior scientists had explored the Glass Fields of Cindara together since they were small. `A1.4`

"Four more samples, and our case is full," Lina said. `A2.1` This was the first mission Dr. Marlow had ever let her lead, and she wanted to bring back every sample on the list. `A2.2` Lava from a new vent could show the scientists in Cinder Hollow what Mount Kettle might do next. `A2.3` Their whole town lived in the mountain's shadow, including their family. `A2.4`

Then the ground shivered under their boots. `A3.1` Teo's meter beeped three times, then three times more. `A3.2` "That's fourteen tremors in the last hour," he said. "Dr. Marlow's rule is to pack up after ten." `A3.3` Lina didn't even look up. "Rules are for people who don't know what they're doing." `A3.4`

Teo's face turned hot. `A4.1` Lina always acted like he was still the little kid tagging along behind her. `A4.2` He remembered Grandma Sol's stories about the Year of Ash, when their old village was buried because people waited too long to leave. `A4.3` "We should go," he said quietly. "Now." `A4.4`

### Passage B · The Last Sample, Part 2 (Short story)

Lina stood up and wiped the gray dust from her gloves. `B1.1` "You want to quit every time things get hard," she snapped. `B1.2` "And you never listen to anybody but yourself!" Teo shot back. `B1.3`

Ash looked from one sibling to the other. `B2.1` Lina had been his best friend since they were six, and he didn't want to take sides against her. `B2.2` But Teo's numbers scared him, and his stomach felt tight. `B2.3` He opened his mouth to speak, then closed it again. `B2.4`

"Ash, tell him we have time," Lina said. `B3.1` Ash stared at the sample case and said nothing. `B3.2` His silence made Lina even more determined, and she marched back to the vent with her steel cup. `B3.3`

Teo would not budge. `B4.1` He was adamant that they leave, no matter how many times Lina told him to calm down. `B4.2` Above them, a thin gray plume began to rise from the top of Mount Kettle. `B4.3`

### Passage C · The Last Sample, Part 3 (Short story)

A deep boom rolled across the Glass Fields. `C1.1` Below them, a crack split the lower trail, and bright orange lava began to pour across it. `C1.2` The lower trail was the easy way back to the hover-sled. `C1.3` Now the only way home was the Obsidian Ridge, a narrow path of sharp black glass. `C1.4`

Ash finally found his voice. `C2.1` "Lina, I'm your friend, and that's why I'm saying this," he said. "Teo is right. We have to go." `C2.2` Lina looked at the half-empty sample case, then at the river of lava. `C2.3` She thought about Dr. Marlow trusting her, and about her little brother standing beside her. `C2.4` Slowly, she snapped the case shut. `C2.5`

"Teo, you studied the ridge map last night," she said. "You lead." `C3.1` Teo blinked in surprise, then nodded. `C3.2` Falling ash stung their eyes as they climbed, and the air smelled like rotten eggs. `C3.3` Teo called out every loose rock, and Lina followed right behind him. `C3.4`

They reached the hover-sled just as a second crack opened on the ridge behind them. `C4.1` If they had stayed ten more minutes, the path would have been gone. `C4.2`

### Passage D · The Last Sample, Part 4 (Short story)

Back in Cinder Hollow, Dr. Marlow studied the sample case. `D1.1` "You only brought back half," she said. "But you brought back all three of you, and that's the part that matters." `D1.2` She held up one sample. "And this lava from the new vent will still help us warn the town." `D1.3`

That night, Lina found Teo on the porch, watching the red glow on the mountain. `D2.1` "I'm sorry I said you quit when things get hard," she said. "You were the brave one today." `D2.2` Teo grinned. "Your samples mattered too. I just wanted you to hear me." `D2.3`

Ash sat down between them. `D3.1` "Next time, I'm not waiting so long to speak up," he said. `D3.2` Lina bumped his shoulder. "Next time, I'll ask both of you before I decide." `D3.3`

The next week, Dr. Marlow gave the team a new mission. `D4.1` This time, Lina and Teo planned it side by side, as partners. `D4.2`

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Seismometers | Volcanologists use tools called seismometers to feel tiny earthquakes. A sudden swarm of small earthquakes can warn that magma is moving under a volcano. |
| 2 | Catching lava | Volcanologists collect hot lava with a steel rod or a rock hammer. Then they dip it in water so it cools fast and keeps a record of what the lava was like. |
| 4 | Silver suits | Volcanologists who work very close to lava sometimes wear shiny silver suits. The silver coating reflects heat away from their bodies. |
| 5 | Warnings that worked | In 1991, scientists warned that Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines was about to erupt. Tens of thousands of people left in time, and thousands of lives were saved. |
| 6 | Tiltmeters | When magma rises inside a volcano, the ground can swell up a tiny bit, like a balloon. Tools called tiltmeters notice these small changes. |
| 8 | Mount St. Helens | When Mount St. Helens in Washington erupted on May 18, 1980, its north side slid away in the largest landslide ever recorded. The mountain lost about 400 meters (1,300 feet) of its height. |
| 10 | Pyroclastic flows | A pyroclastic flow is a fast avalanche of hot ash, rock, and gas that rushes down a volcano. It can move faster than a car on a highway, so people must leave early instead of trying to outrun it. |
| 11 | The first volcano observatory | The world's first volcano observatory was built on Mount Vesuvius in Italy in 1841. Scientists have been watching the volcano from there ever since. |
| 13 | The loudest boom | When the Krakatoa volcano in Indonesia erupted in 1883, the boom was heard about 4,800 kilometers (3,000 miles) away. It may be the loudest sound in recorded history. |
| 15 | Volcano smells | Volcanoes give off gases you can't always see. One of them, hydrogen sulfide, smells like rotten eggs, so volcano scientists often carry gas meters to stay safe. |

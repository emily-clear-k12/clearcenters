# The Launch Schedule — Content v1 (MA.3.7C-XP)

**Grade 3 · Math · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/launch-schedule.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.7C · **Also shows up under:** 3.4A

**I can:** I can add and subtract time in minutes using a time line. I can find when something starts, when it ends, and how long it lasts.

**Mission:** Plan every launch, flight, and supply run on the Sky Reef by adding and subtracting minutes.

**Story:** It's Launch Day on Cloudreach! Sky-ships, gliders, and supply runs are lined up, and the crew needs a schedule that runs right to the minute.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Launch board (starts at 15%) · Flights cleared · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Why 60 minutes? | Long ago, people in ancient Babylon counted in groups of 60. We still use their idea today: 60 seconds make a minute, and 60 minutes make an hour. |
| 2 | Sundials | Before clocks, people used sundials. A sundial has a stick that makes a shadow. As the Sun moves across the sky, the shadow moves and points to the time. |
| 3 | Sand timers | An hourglass is a sand timer. Sand falls from the top glass to the bottom glass. Long ago, sailors turned hourglasses over and over to keep track of time at sea. |
| 5 | The first balloon riders | In 1783, two people rode a hot-air balloon over Paris, France. It was the first time people flew in a balloon that was not tied down. The trip lasted about 25 minutes. |
| 6 | Pendulum clocks | In 1656, a scientist named Christiaan Huygens built a clock with a swinging weight called a pendulum. It kept time much better than the clocks before it. |
| 7 | The glider man | In the 1890s, Otto Lilienthal of Germany made about 2,000 flights in gliders he built himself. His flights helped later inventors learn how wings work. |
| 9 | a.m. and p.m. | The letters a.m. come from Latin words that mean "before midday." The letters p.m. mean "after midday." At 12 noon, the clock switches from a.m. to p.m. |
| 10 | Atomic clocks | Atomic clocks are the most exact clocks people have built. The best ones would lose less than one second in millions of years. |
| 11 | Time zones | Earth is split into time zones. When it is morning in one part of the world, it can be nighttime on the other side. |
| 12 | Big Ben | Many people call the famous clock tower in London, England, "Big Ben." But Big Ben is really the name of the giant bell inside the tower. |

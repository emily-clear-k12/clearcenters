# The Missing Map — Content v1 (ELA.3.6F-XP)

**Grade 3 · ELAR · Frostveil.** Written Sept 26, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/missing-map.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.6F · **Also shows up under:** 3.7C, 3.7B, 3.3B, 3.8A, 3.8C, 3.11D

**I can:** I can make inferences and prove them with clues from the text. I can fix mistakes in a note or report.

**Mission:** Read the crew's logs, notes, and reports to figure out what happened to the map, then send the report to Mission Control.

**Story:** The night before a big trek on icy Frostveil, the crew's map disappears. Tiny wet spots, a torn corner, and a trail of prints are the only clues.

**Meters on Frostveil:** Trail map (starts at 10%) · Clues · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega on Frostveil. We have a mystery. Tomorrow the crew hikes to the Glass Falls, but this morning our map was gone. Nobody knows where it went. The clues are in our logs and notes. Read carefully and prove every idea. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · Commander Vega's Log, Morning (Log (informational narrative))

Tomorrow the crew plans to hike to the Glass Falls. `A1.1` Snow covers the trail, so it is easy to get lost. `A1.2` Our map is printed on soft blue cloth so it will not rip in the wind. `A1.3` We cannot go without it. `A1.4`

This morning, the map was gone. `A2.1` Its tube was lying on the floor under the window. `A2.2` The tube was empty, and the cap had rolled under a chair. `A2.3`

The window was open a crack, and the room felt as cold as a freezer. `A3.1` I found three tiny wet spots on the windowsill. `A3.2` Each spot was shaped like a little flower with three petals. `A3.3`

When I asked about the map, Kai looked down at his boots. `A4.1` His cheeks turned pink. `A4.2` "I think this might be my fault," he said quietly. `A4.3`

### Passage B · Kai's note to Vega (Note (has mistakes))

Vega, I am so sorry about the map. I looked at it late last **nite**`[B:nite]` by the window. Then I rolled it up and put it back in the tube. The heater **were**`[B:were]` too hot, so I opened the window a crack. **i**`[B:i]` heard a soft scratching sound, but I thought it was only the wind. I forgot to close the window before I went to sleep.

### Passage C · Nova's Field Notes: Frostkits (Informational text)

Frostkits are small snow animals that live near our outpost. `C1.1` They are about the size of a house cat. `C1.2` Their thick white fur helps them hide in the snow. `C1.3`

A frostkit has three toes on each paw. `C2.1` Its footprints look like tiny flowers pressed into the snow. `C2.2`

Frostkits are curious and gentle. `C3.1` They sleep in the daytime and explore at night. `C3.2` Most of the time, they stay far away from people. `C3.3`

In the cold season, a mother frostkit digs a den deep inside a snowdrift. `C4.1` She lines the den with soft, dry things, like moss, feathers, and scraps of cloth. `C4.2` A soft lining keeps her babies warm. `C4.3`

### Passage D · Kai's Report: The Torn Corner (Report (description))

At sunrise, Nova found something half buried in the snow. `D1.1` It was one corner of our map, about as big as my hand. `D1.2`

The piece shows a small square for the outpost. `D2.1` A dotted line leaves the square and heads north. `D2.2` Next to the line, someone had written, "Stay on the trail past Ridge Rock." `D2.3`

The edges of the piece are ragged, not straight like a cut from scissors. `D3.1` Along one side, Nova counted six tiny bite marks. `D3.2`

The piece was lying about twenty steps from the window. `D4.1` Next to it, a line of small flower-shaped prints led away across the snow. `D4.2` The prints ended at the big snowdrift behind Ridge Rock. `D4.3`

### Passage E · Nova's Log, Ridge Rock (Log (narrative))

We followed the prints to the snowdrift behind Ridge Rock. `E1.1` Near the bottom, we found a round hole, just big enough for a small animal. `E1.2`

Kai shined a dim light inside. `E2.1` A mother frostkit blinked back at us. `E2.2` Three tiny kits were curled up beside her, fast asleep. `E2.3`

Under the kits, I could see a patch of blue cloth with dotted lines on it. `E3.1` Kai let out a long breath. `E3.2` "Well, we found our map," he whispered. `E3.3`

Commander Vega shook her head, and then she smiled. `E4.1` "The kits need it more than we do," she said softly. `E4.2` We tiptoed away, and nobody said a word until we were far from the den. `E4.3`

That night, I drew a new map from memory and from the torn corner. `E5.1` Kai closed the window and checked the latch twice. `E5.2`

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Snowflakes | Most snowflakes have six sides or six points. Each one grows in its own way as it falls. |
| 4 | Arctic foxes | Arctic foxes grow thick white fur in winter. In summer, their fur turns brown or gray. |
| 5 | Animal tracks | Scientists can tell which animal walked by looking at the shape and size of its tracks in snow or mud. |
| 6 | Snow dens | Snow has lots of air trapped inside it. That air holds in heat, so a den dug in snow can stay much warmer than the wind outside. |
| 8 | North on maps | On most maps, north is at the top. A compass rose shows which way is north, south, east, and west. |
| 9 | Polar bear paws | Polar bears have fur on the bottoms of their paws. It helps them grip the ice and keeps their feet warm. |
| 10 | Clear ice, white snow | A single ice crystal is clear like glass. A pile of snow looks white because light bounces around all the tiny crystals. |
| 11 | Polar bear cubs | Polar bear cubs are born in snow dens in the middle of winter. They stay inside with their mother for a few months. |
| 13 | The North Star | Long ago, travelers used the North Star to find which way was north. It stays in almost the same spot in the night sky. |
| 15 | Compasses | A compass needle points north because Earth acts like a giant magnet. |

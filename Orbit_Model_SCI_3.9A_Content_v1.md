# The Orbit Model — Content v1 (SCI.3.9A-XP)

**Grade 3 · Science · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/orbit-model.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.9A · **Also shows up under:** 3.9B, 3.1E, 3.1G, 3.2B, 3.2C, 3.3A, 3.3B, 3.3C, 3.5A, 3.5D, 3.5F

**I can:** I can build a model and explain how Earth orbits the Sun and the Moon orbits Earth. I can put the planets in order from the Sun and tell how a model is different from real life.

**Mission:** Study the Sun, Earth, and Moon, order the planets, and build an orbit model you can explain.

**Story:** The crew uses the sky reef's big telescope to study Earth's Sun, Earth, and Moon. Then they build an orbit model to show the whole reef how they move.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Orbit model (starts at 15%) · Model parts · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The heavy Sun | The Sun holds more than 99 out of every 100 parts of all the stuff (mass) in our whole solar system. Its pull keeps the planets in their orbits. |
| 2 | The longest trip | Neptune is so far from the Sun that it takes about 165 Earth years to go around the Sun just one time. |
| 3 | Speedy space station | The International Space Station orbits Earth about every 90 minutes. The astronauts on board see about 16 sunrises every day. |
| 6 | How far is the Moon? | The Moon is about 239,000 miles from Earth. About 30 Earths could fit side by side in the space between them. |
| 7 | A big idea | In 1543, a scientist named Nicolaus Copernicus wrote that Earth and the other planets travel around the Sun. Many people back then thought the Sun went around Earth. |
| 8 | The Moon's same face | The Moon spins one time for each trip around Earth. That's why we always see the same side of the Moon from Earth. |
| 10 | People on the Moon | In 1969, astronauts from the Apollo 11 mission became the first people to walk on the Moon. Their footprints are still there, because the Moon has no wind to blow them away. |
| 11 | A giant Sun | The Sun is so big that more than one million Earths could fit inside it. |
| 13 | Leap years | Earth's trip around the Sun really takes about 365 and a quarter days. So every four years, calendars add one extra day, February 29. That year is called a leap year. |
| 15 | The hottest planet | Venus is the hottest planet, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun. Venus has thick clouds and air that trap the Sun's heat like a blanket. |

# The Food Chain Crew — Content v1 (SCI.3.12B-XP)

**Grade 3 · Science · Solara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/food-chain-crew.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.12B · **Also shows up under:** 3.12C, 3.1A, 3.1G, 3.2B, 3.2C, 3.3A, 3.3C, 3.5B, 3.5D, 3.5E, 3.5G

**I can:** I can describe how energy flows from the Sun through a food chain, draw the arrows the right way, and predict what happens when a living thing is removed or when a flood or drought changes an ecosystem.

**Mission:** Build food chains, follow the Sun's energy, and predict what happens when a food chain changes.

**Story:** From the treetop observatory on Solara, the crew points a telescope at Earth. They study real ponds, grasslands, fields, and oceans to follow the energy in food chains.

**Meters on Solara:** Energy flow (starts at 20%) · Food chains · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. Our observatory sits high in the trees of Solara's jungle. Today our big telescope is pointed at Earth. We're studying real food chains there: ponds, grasslands, fields, and oceans. Follow the energy from the Sun to the last eater, and predict what happens when a chain changes. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Earthworm helpers | Earthworms eat dead leaves and bits of soil. Their waste, called castings, makes the soil richer so plants grow better. |
| 2 | Sunlight's trip | Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to travel from the Sun to Earth. The Sun is about 93 million miles away! |
| 3 | Frog tongues | Many frogs catch insects with a long, sticky tongue. It flips out from the front of the mouth and snaps back in less than a second. |
| 5 | Dragonfly nymphs | Young dragonflies, called nymphs, live underwater in ponds for months or even years. They hunt tadpoles and tiny fish before they climb out and grow wings. |
| 8 | Busy bees | Bees visit flowers for nectar and pollen. As they fly from flower to flower, they carry pollen that helps plants make fruit and seeds, like apples. |
| 10 | Kelp forest guards | Sea otters eat lots of sea urchins. Where otters disappear, urchins can eat whole kelp forests. Where otters return, the kelp can grow back. |
| 12 | Fire ant rafts | When a flood comes, fire ants hook their bodies together into a floating raft. The raft can float for days until it reaches dry land. |
| 13 | Hidden fungus | A mushroom is only a small part of a fungus. Most of the fungus is hidden as thin threads spreading through soil or dead wood. |
| 14 | Owl pellets | Owls often swallow small animals whole. Later they cough up a pellet of fur and bones. Scientists study pellets to learn what owls eat. |
| 15 | Deep grass roots | Some prairie grasses have roots deeper than a grown-up is tall. The roots stay alive in a drought, and the grass grows back when the rain returns. |

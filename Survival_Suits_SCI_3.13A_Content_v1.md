# The Survival Suits — Content v1 (SCI.3.13A-XP)

**Grade 3 · Science · Frostveil.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/survival-suits.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.13A · **Also shows up under:** 3.1A, 3.1B, 3.1E, 3.1G, 3.2B, 3.2C, 3.2D, 3.3A, 3.3C, 3.5A, 3.5F

**I can:** I can explain how animal body parts, like a giraffe's long neck or a duck's webbed feet, help animals survive where they live. I can use those ideas to design a survival suit.

**Mission:** Study animal body structures, test models, and design survival suits that copy what animals do to survive.

**Story:** The crew needs survival suits for Frostveil's ice. To design them, they study how real Earth animals survive in the Arctic, the desert, the ocean, and the rainforest.

**Meters on Frostveil:** Suits ready (starts at 15%) · Test swatches · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Waterproof ducks | Ducks have a special spot near their tails that makes oil. They spread the oil over their feathers with their beaks, so water rolls right off. |
| 2 | Giraffe necks | A giraffe's neck can be taller than a grown-up person. But it has only 7 neck bones, the same number as your neck. Each bone is just much longer. |
| 3 | Camel sand shields | Camels can close their nostrils to keep out blowing sand. They also have two rows of long eyelashes that help protect their eyes. |
| 5 | Fennec foxes | Fennec foxes are the smallest foxes, but their ears are huge for their size. The ears let off heat, and they can even hear bugs moving under the sand. |
| 6 | Baby giraffes | A baby giraffe is about 6 feet (almost 2 meters) tall on the day it is born. That's taller than most grown-ups! It can stand up about an hour later. |
| 8 | Bowhead whales | Bowhead whales live in icy Arctic water. They have the thickest blubber of any animal. It can be thicker than a school ruler is long! |
| 10 | Sea otter fur | Sea otters have the thickest fur of any animal. A patch the size of a postage stamp can have up to a million hairs! |
| 11 | Copying nature | When people copy ideas from animals or plants to design things, it's called biomimicry. Bumps on humpback whale flippers gave engineers ideas for better fan blades. |
| 13 | Musk ox hair | Musk oxen have some of the longest hair of any wild animal. Their long outer hair can hang almost to the ground, with soft, warm wool underneath. |
| 15 | Hollow hair | Reindeer have hollow hairs filled with air. The air helps hold in body heat, and it even helps reindeer float when they swim across rivers. |

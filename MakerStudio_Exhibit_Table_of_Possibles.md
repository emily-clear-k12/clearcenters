# Maker Studio · Exhibit Mode — Table of Possibles

**Sept 24, 2026. Draft for Emily's review.** One strong example exhibit for **each of the 5 exhibit types, in each subject, at each grade**: 5 × 4 × 3 = **60 exhibits**. The point is to show how the five types differ, and to lay a foundation that more content can be added to later, one standard at a time.

Nothing here is authored or built. Every row is a sketch: a standard, a driving question, and enough strong pieces and traps to show the case would work.

**Where the standards come from.** Math and ELAR are quoted from Emily's TEKS PDFs (verified Sept 20, 2026, supplied here Sept 24). Science was checked code by code against Texas Gateway on Sept 24. Social Studies codes come from `BRIEFINGS-ASSIGNMENT-TABLE.md` (built from the 2022 SS TEKS). Wording is shortened in the tables; check the full text again when a case is authored (convention #11).

## 1 · The five types, one more time

| Type | The question it answers | Wall | What's checked besides the picks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prove It** | "Is this true? Show me." | One wall, plus a centerpiece if Emily keeps it | Every piece supports the claim |
| **Side by Side** | "How are these alike and different?" | A split wall: left, right, and a "both" spot | Each piece is on the correct side |
| **Then to Now** | "How did this change, and in what order?" | A timeline wall | **The order** |
| **Every Kind** | "What does ___ look like in the world?" | A gallery wall, with one spot for "the tricky one" | Every piece truly belongs; the edge case is placed and explained |
| **Portrait** | "Who or what is this, and why does it matter?" | A center portrait with pieces around it | Every piece tells something true and important about the subject |

**Wall size:** grade 3 = 4, grade 4 = 5, grade 5 = 6 (Emily, Sept 24). The storage room always has one more strong piece than the wall holds, plus 1 weak piece and 3–4 traps.

**The trap kinds** (used in the tables below): **Myth** (a common wrong idea that looks official) · **Wrong place** (real, but for a different place, time or topic) · **Not the job** (true and related, but not the kind of evidence the standard asks for) · **Misleading picture** · **Wrong side** (Side by Side only: a true fact placed on the wrong half) · **Out of order** (Then to Now only: a real step with a date or detail that gives away where it really goes).

**"Bank"** = how many images the site already has filed under that standard code (counted Sept 24). A number means the bank has a head start. **0** means all new art. **Drawn** means the engine should draw the pieces itself (see §6).

## 6 · Notes that shape the build

**Math pieces should be drawn by the engine, not generated as images.** An image tool can't be trusted to draw 3/4 of a bar exactly. A fraction bar, number line, array or coordinate grid can be described in the case file as data (`{ kind: "fractionBar", parts: 4, shaded: 3, width: "full" }`) and drawn as SVG by the engine. That makes every math piece exact, keeps the traps precise (the "different-size wholes" trap is just two widths), and costs no art at all. Photos are only needed for real-world pieces like the pumpkin on a scale or a stop sign.

**ELAR needs an original passage per case.** That's the main authoring cost for ELAR. Passages must be original or public domain (convention #8). *The Cracked Pot* is a traditional tale and should be a fresh retelling. The Galveston 1900 case must use real public-domain sources or clearly labeled adaptations.

**Every exhibit also covers two "ride-along" standards.** From Emily's PDFs:
- ELAR research strand: **3.13C / 4.13C / 5.13C** "identify and gather relevant information from a variety of sources" and **3.13H / 4.13H / 5.13H** "use an appropriate mode of delivery, whether written, oral, or multimodal, to present results." Every exhibit, in every subject, practices both.
- Math process standard: **3.1G / 4.1G / 5.1G** "display, explain, and justify mathematical ideas and arguments using precise mathematical language in written or oral communication." Every math exhibit's plaque practices this.

**Rows that need a careful human read before authoring:** Science 5.12B (use only the well-supported claims about the Yellowstone wolves) · SS 3.10B (Juneteenth and Cinco de Mayo, handled accurately and respectfully) · SS 5.9A (plantations and enslaved labor, stated plainly, not softened) · ELAR 5.10G (stereotyping examples must teach the harm without repeating it carelessly).

**How the five types relate to other centers.** Every Kind sits closest to Classification Lab, so those cases should share topics but not pieces. Then to Now overlaps with Assembly Deck's ordering; the difference is that the exhibit picks *which* steps belong before ordering them.

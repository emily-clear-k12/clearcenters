## Passages

### Passage A: Nova's Field Log, Day 1

*Informational narrative*

Something is wrong in the canopy. For as long as the crew has lived on Lumara, the glow-moths have lit up the treetops every night. Tonight I counted only six glowing moths. Last month I counted more than two hundred.

I checked the moth traps three times. Each time, my hands shook a little more. The moths were there, sitting on the leaves, but their wings stayed dark.

Glow-moths do not glow to light up the forest. They glow to find each other. A moth flashes its wings, and another moth flashes back. That is how they meet and build nests together. A moth that cannot be seen is a moth that is alone.

Kai says the new floodlights by the landing pad make the outpost safer at night. They are so bright that I can read my notes without a lamp, even at midnight. They switched on for the first time eleven days ago.

Tomorrow I will walk the canopy trail again. I hope I am wrong about what I am starting to think.

### Passage C: The Lantern Keeper

*Legend (traditional tale)*

Long ago, in the high branches of Lumara, lived a keeper named Oru. Every evening, Oru climbed from tree to tree and lit a hundred lanterns. "No one in my forest will ever be lost in the dark," he said proudly.

The lanterns burned all night. Travelers praised Oru, and his chest swelled like a seed pod in the rain.

But season after season, the forest grew quieter. The night songs of the tree frogs faded. The glow-moths, which once danced like sparks, hid under the leaves. Oru did not notice. He was too busy polishing lanterns.

One night, a small voice called from a branch. It was an old glow-moth, her wings as dim as ash. "Keeper," she said, "your light is kind, but it is too loud. We cannot hear each other's light."

Oru laughed. "Light does not make noise, little one."

"Then blow out your lanterns for one night," said the moth, "and listen."

Oru frowned. He was not used to taking advice from a moth. Still, his steps were light as he moved from tree to tree, blowing out each flame.

Darkness fell over the canopy. For a long moment, nothing happened. Then, far away, one tiny spark flashed. Another answered it. Then ten, then a hundred, until the whole forest shimmered like a river of stars.

Oru sat in the dark and smiled. From that night on, he lit only one lantern, low and small, and let the forest keep the rest of the night for itself.

### Passage D: Nova's Field Log, Day 5

*Informational narrative with data*

Today we ran a test. With Commander Vega's permission, Kai turned off the floodlights on the east side of the outpost from sunset to sunrise. The west side stayed lit, just like before. Everything else stayed the same. Both sides have the same trees, the same flowers, and the same number of moth traps.

At midnight I counted glowing moths on both sides. East side (lights off): 58 glowing moths. West side (lights on): 4 glowing moths.

Kai stood next to me on the east trail and didn't say anything for a long time. Then he said, "I misjudged the lights. I'll talk to Vega about new ones." He sounded disappointed, but he didn't argue.

There may be other reasons, too. We only tested one night. Tomorrow we will switch sides to make sure the results are not about the trees.

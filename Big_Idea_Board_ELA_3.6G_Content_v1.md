# The Big Idea Board — Content v1 (ELA.3.6G-XP)

**Grade 3 · ELAR · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/big-idea-board.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.6G · **Also shows up under:** 3.7D, 3.9D, 3.6F, 3.7C, 3.3B, 3.11D

**I can:** I can tell important details from interesting extras to find the key idea. I can retell and summarize a text in order.

**Mission:** Read Nova's entries about manta rays, monarchs, Arctic terns, and flying fish. Sort key ideas from fun extras, retell in order, and fill the Big Idea Board.

**Story:** The Sky Reef on Cloudreach is opening to visitors. Nova has written field-guide entries about Earth's amazing sea and sky creatures, but the Big Idea Board is empty.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Board filled (starts at 5%) · Idea cards · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

## Passages

### Passage A · Nova's Field Guide: Manta Rays (Informational text)

Manta rays are huge fish that glide through the ocean the way birds glide through the sky. `A1.1` A giant manta ray can be more than 20 feet wide from fin tip to fin tip. `A1.2` That is wider than a car is long. `A1.3` Its big, flat fins flap slowly, like wings. `A1.4`

Manta rays are giants, but they eat some of the tiniest food in the sea. `A2.1` Their food is plankton, tiny animals that drift in the water. `A2.2` A manta swims with its mouth wide open, and two flaps on its head steer the plankton inside. `A2.3` Its gills catch the food like a strainer, and the water flows back out. `A2.4`

Manta rays keep clean with help from small fish. `A3.1` They visit spots on coral reefs called cleaning stations. `A3.2` There, small fish nibble dead skin and tiny pests off the manta's body. `A3.3` The small fish get a meal, and the manta gets clean. `A3.4`

Sometimes a manta ray leaps right out of the water and lands with a big splash. `A4.1` Scientists are still not sure why they do it. `A4.2`

### Passage B · Nova's Field Guide: Monarch Butterflies (Informational text)

Monarch butterflies are orange and black insects that make an amazing journey. `B1.1` Every fall, millions of monarchs fly south to get away from the cold. `B1.2` Some fly up to 3,000 miles to reach forests in the mountains of Mexico. `B1.3` There, they rest together in huge groups that cover whole trees. `B1.4`

A monarch's life has four stages. `B2.1` It starts as a tiny egg on a milkweed leaf. `B2.2` The egg hatches into a caterpillar with yellow, black, and white stripes. `B2.3` The caterpillar eats and grows, then hangs upside down and forms a green case called a chrysalis. `B2.4` About two weeks later, an adult butterfly comes out. `B2.5`

Milkweed helps keep monarchs safe. `B3.1` Milkweed is the only plant monarch caterpillars eat. `B3.2` The plant has a bitter poison that does not hurt the caterpillar. `B3.3` The poison stays in the monarch's body, so a bird that tries to eat one gets a nasty taste. `B3.4` A monarch's bright orange wings warn birds to stay away. `B3.5`

In spring, the monarchs start flying north again. `B4.1` Along the way, they lay eggs, and then they die. `B4.2` Their children and grandchildren keep flying north. `B4.3` It takes several generations of monarchs to finish the trip. `B4.4`

### Passage C · Nova's Field Guide: Arctic Terns (Informational text)

The Arctic tern is a small seabird with a black cap and a bright red beak. `C1.1` It is not much bigger than a pigeon. `C1.2` But this little bird holds a giant record. `C1.3`

Every year, the Arctic tern flies from the far north of Earth to the far south, and then back again. `C2.1` It spends summer near the North Pole. `C2.2` Then it flies all the way to Antarctica, where summer is just starting. `C2.3` The round trip can add up to about 44,000 miles in one year. `C2.4` No other animal on Earth migrates farther. `C2.5`

Because it chases summer from one end of Earth to the other, the Arctic tern sees more daylight than any other animal. `C3.1`

Arctic terns can live for 30 years. `C4.1` Over a lifetime, one tern may fly far enough to go to the Moon and back about three times! `C4.2` Terns catch fish by diving headfirst into the water. `C4.3`

### Passage D · Nova's Field Guide: Flying Fish (Informational text)

Flying fish cannot really fly like birds. `D1.1` Instead, they leap out of the ocean and glide through the air to get away from hungry hunters. `D1.2`

When a tuna or a dolphin chases it, a flying fish swims fast toward the surface. `D2.1` It bursts out of the water and spreads its long fins like wings. `D2.2` Its tail slaps the water again and again to push it higher. `D2.3` Then it glides above the waves, far away from the hunter. `D2.4` At last, it drops back into the sea. `D2.5`

A flying fish can glide more than 600 feet in one trip. `D3.1` That is about as long as two football fields. `D3.2`

Flying fish live in warm oceans all over the world. `D4.1` At night, bright lights attract them. `D4.2` Sometimes they glide right onto the decks of boats! `D4.3`

### Passage E · Kai's Summary: Flying Fish (Summary (has a problem))

Flying fish live in warm oceans. `E1.1` At night, they like bright lights, and sometimes they land on boats. `E1.2` They have long fins. `E1.3`

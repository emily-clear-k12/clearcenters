# The Decimal Dials — Content v1 (MA.4.2F-XP)

**Grade 4 · Math · Mechara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/decimal-dials.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.2F · **Also shows up under:** 4.2E, 4.2G, 4.2H

**I can:** I can compare and order decimals to the hundredths using grids, number lines, and money, and match decimals to fractions.

**Mission:** Tune the robot factory's decimal dials so every machine runs at just the right setting.

**Story:** The Gearworks Factory on Mechara runs on decimal dials. The crew needs help reading, comparing, and ordering tenths and hundredths to tune every machine.

**Meters on Mechara:** Factory power (starts at 15%) · Gears tuned · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega. We're at the Gearworks Factory on Mechara, the robot city. Every machine here runs on a decimal dial. The dials show tenths and hundredths, and some are set all wrong. Help Kai and Nova read, compare, and order the dials so the factory runs smoothly. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |

| 1 | Where "dime" comes from | The word dime comes from an old French word that means one tenth. A dime is one tenth of a dollar! |
| 2 | Meter sticks | A meter stick has 100 centimeters. So each centimeter is one hundredth of a meter, or 0.01 meter. |
| 3 | What's in a penny | A U.S. penny made since 1982 is mostly zinc inside. Only a thin coat on the outside is copper. |
| 5 | Gear teeth | Gears are wheels with teeth. When two gears fit together, one turns one way and the other turns the opposite way. |
| 6 | Welding robots | Many car factories use robot arms to weld metal parts together. A robot can make the same weld over and over without getting tired. |
| 8 | Hundredths of a second | In swimming races, times are measured to the hundredth of a second. Some races are won by just 0.01 second! |
| 10 | The moving assembly line | In 1913, a car factory started using a moving assembly line. Building one car went from more than 12 hours to about 1 and a half hours. |
| 11 | Robot vacuums | Many robot vacuums can find their way back to their charging dock by themselves when their battery gets low. |
| 12 | Robot distance sensors | Some robots send out sound or light and time how long it takes to bounce back. That tells them how far away things are. |
| 13 | Ramps are machines | A ramp is a simple machine. It lets you raise a heavy load by pushing it a longer way with less force. |

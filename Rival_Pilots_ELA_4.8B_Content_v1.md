# The Rival Pilots — Content v1 (ELA.4.8B-XP)

**Grade 4 · ELAR · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/rival-pilots.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.8B · **Also shows up under:** 4.8D, 4.8C, 4.6F, 4.7C, 4.3B, 4.11D

**I can:** I can explain how characters act toward each other and how they change, using evidence. I can explain how the setting shapes what happens.

**Mission:** Read the story of the Sky Race, a crew log, and a pilot's journal to explain how the two rivals change, then send the crew's report.

**Story:** It's race day on Cloudreach. Two young sky-skiff pilots, one proud and one shy, can't stand each other. Then a storm sweeps across the Coral Clouds course.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Tailwind (starts at 25%) · Pilot badges · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega at the Cloudreach launch dock. It's Sky Race day! Two young pilots are the talk of the dock. Mira Dash is the champ, and she knows it. Tobin Reyes barely says a word. The race story is coming in over the radio in parts. Read closely. These two are about to surprise everyone. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The Sky Race, Part 1: The Launch Dock (Realistic fiction (story, part 1))

Every year, pilots from all over Cloudreach gather for the Sky Race. `A1.1` The course winds through the Coral Clouds, where giant reefs float on the wind like huge sponges. `A1.2` Between the reefs run gust lanes, narrow rivers of fast wind that can push a sky-skiff twice as fast or flip it upside down. `A1.3`

Mira Dash had won the junior race two years in a row. `A2.1` She strutted across the launch dock with her goggles pushed up on her head, waving at the crowd. `A2.2` "I don't need to study the wind," she told everyone. "The wind should study me!" `A2.3`

At the far end of the dock, Tobin Reyes checked the straps on his skiff for the third time. `A3.1` He had drawn a careful map of every gust lane on the course. `A3.2` When a reporter asked him a question, his face turned red, and he only nodded. `A3.3`

Mira hurried past and bumped his map with her elbow. `A4.1` It fluttered off the dock and sank into the clouds below. `A4.2` "Oops," she said, and she laughed. `A4.3` Tobin opened his mouth, then closed it again. `A4.4` He said nothing at all. `A4.5`

### Passage B · The Sky Race, Part 2: The Storm (Realistic fiction (story, part 2))

The starting horn blew, and twelve skiffs shot into the sky. `B1.1` Mira dove straight into the fastest gust lane. `B1.2` She zoomed ahead of everyone, whooping into the wind. `B1.3`

Tobin stayed in the slow, steady air along the edge of the reef. `B2.1` Without his map, he had to fly by memory, watching which way the cloud-coral leaned. `B2.2` When the coral leans hard, the wind is strong, he reminded himself. `B2.3`

Halfway through the course, the sky turned the dark purple of a bruise. `B3.1` The air grew cold, and the cloud-coral began to shake. `B3.2` A storm was rolling in from the west. `B3.3` The judges' horn blew three times, the signal for every pilot to land at once. `B3.4`

Mira did not turn back. `B4.1` "A little wind can't stop a champion," she said. `B4.2` She pushed deeper into the gust lane just as the storm swept into it. `B4.3` A wall of wind slammed her skiff sideways, and her steering fin snapped. `B4.4`

### Passage C · The Sky Race, Part 3: Under the Reef (Realistic fiction (story, part 3))

Tobin saw Mira's skiff spinning toward the dark reef. `C1.1` Every part of him wanted to fly back to the safe, quiet dock. `C1.2` Instead, he turned his skiff toward the storm. `C1.3`

He pulled up beside her and tossed her a tow line. `C2.1` "Hold on!" Mira shouted. "I'll steer us out through the gust lane!" `C2.2` "No!" Tobin shouted back, louder than he had ever spoken in his life. `C2.3` "The storm is inside the gust lane. We have to go under the reef, where the air is calm!" `C2.4`

Mira stared at him. `C3.1` For once, she did not argue. `C3.2` "Okay," she said. "You lead." `C3.3`

Tobin guided them down beneath the floating reef. `C4.1` The thick coral blocked the worst of the wind like a giant umbrella. `C4.2` Slowly and carefully, the two skiffs drifted home through the calm air below. `C4.3`

### Passage D · The Sky Race, Part 4: The Next Morning (Realistic fiction (story, part 4))

The judges stopped the race when the storm hit, so no one won the Sky Race that year. `D1.1`

The next morning, Mira found Tobin at the launch dock. `D2.1` She held out a new map, one she had drawn herself. `D2.2` "I asked the old pilots about the wind all night," she said. "I should have listened to you. And I'm sorry about your map." `D2.3`

Tobin smiled. `D3.1` When the reporter came by, he did not just nod this time. `D3.2` "Mira is the bravest pilot I know," he said in a clear voice. "Next year, we're going to study the course together." `D3.3`

Mira grinned. `D4.1` "And next year," she added, "I'm still going to win." `D4.2`

### Passage E · Tobin's Pilot Journal (Journal entry)

Today I shouted at the fastest pilot in Cloudreach. `E1.1` I didn't plan to. `E1.2` The words just burst out, because Mira was about to fly straight into the storm. `E1.3`

My whole life, I've stayed quiet even when I knew the answer. `E2.1` I was afraid people would laugh at me. `E2.2` Today I learned that sometimes staying quiet is the dangerous choice. `E2.3`

Mira listened to me. `E3.1` I think that surprised both of us. `E3.2`

### Passage F · Nova's Crew Log: The Coral Clouds Course (Crew log (informational))

The Sky Race course runs through the Coral Clouds, the thickest reefs on Cloudreach. `F1.1` Pilots have raced here for more than a hundred years, and the old pilots still teach the young ones how to read the wind. `F1.2`

On warm afternoons, warm air rises off the reefs, and storms often build on the west side of the course. `F2.1` That is why the race usually starts early in the morning. `F2.2` This year, a broken starting horn delayed the race until the middle of the afternoon. `F2.3`

Under the biggest reefs, the air stays much calmer, even in a storm. `F3.1` Old pilots call that space the quiet shelf. `F3.2` A pilot who knows the course can hide there until the wind dies down. `F3.3`

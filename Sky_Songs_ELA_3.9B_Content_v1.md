# The Sky Songs — Content v1 (ELA.3.9B-XP)

**Grade 3 · ELAR · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/sky-songs.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.9B · **Also shows up under:** 3.10D, 3.6F, 3.3B, 3.12A, 3.11D

**I can:** I can explain rhyme schemes, sound devices, and stanzas in poems. I can tell how a poet's words help me see and hear.

**Mission:** Count stanzas and lines, find rhyme schemes and sound words, and add two lines of your own to the Song Shell Wall.

**Story:** On Cloudreach, the crew holds a song night on the sky reef. Nova, Kai, Vega, and a reef-keeper share poems about humming cloud whales, jingling reef bells, and sky rays that fly like kites.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Reef chorus (starts at 20%) · Song shells · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega on the sky reef of Cloudreach. Tonight the reef-keepers invited our crew to song night. Each of us wrote a poem to share. On Cloudreach, a poem is only finished when someone explains how it was built. Help us do that. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The Cloud Whales (Poem by Nova (rhyming))

The cloud whales drift so slow and wide, `A1.1` with puffs of fog on every side. `A1.2` They sing a song as deep as the sea. `A1.3` Hooom, hooom, it rumbles down to me. `A1.4`

Their bellies shine like pearls so white. `A2.1` Their fins glow softly in the night. `A2.2` They circle round the coral towers, `A2.3` then nap on clouds for hours and hours. `A2.4`

Goodnight, cloud whales, big and gray. `A3.1` Come back to sing another day! `A3.2`

### Passage B · Wind Chime Reef (Poem by Kai (rhyming))

The reef bells jingle, ding-a-ding. `B1.1` They twinkle in the morning light. `B1.2` Then all the sky crabs start to sing. `B1.3` Click-clack, click-clack, left and right! `B1.4`

Bubble-birds bounce by in a bunch. `B2.1` Pop, pop, pop go the bubbles they blow. `B2.2` They stop at the reef to munch their lunch, `B2.3` then zip away with a whoosh below. `B2.4`

### Passage C · Up Here (Poem by Commander Vega (free verse))

Up here, the sky is my backyard. `C1.1` Up here, the clouds brush my cheeks, cool and damp. `C1.2` Up here, the reef glows pink at sunrise. `C1.3`

Slowly, slowly, the sun climbs over the coral. `C2.1` Far below, the ocean looks small enough to hold. `C2.2` But up here, with my crew, `C2.3` I feel big and brave and free. `C2.4`

### Passage D · The Sky Ray Song (Song sung by Reef-keeper Tamsin of Cloudreach)

Flap, flap, the sky rays fly `D1.1` across the purple evening sky. `D1.2` Their wings spread wide like giant kites. `D1.3` They swoop and swirl to dizzy heights. `D1.4`

Sing high, sing low, `D2.1` where the warm winds blow! `D2.2`

They dip and dive through clouds of cream. `D3.1` They skim across a misty stream. `D3.2` The little ones hold on so tight `D3.3` and ride their mothers through the night. `D3.4`

Sing high, sing low, `D4.1` where the warm winds blow! `D4.2`

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Whale songs | Male humpback whales sing long songs. One song can last up to 20 minutes, and a whale may sing it over and over for hours. |
| 2 | Echoes | An echo is a sound that bounces back to you. It happens when sound hits something hard, like a cliff or a canyon wall. |
| 3 | How pearls form | A pearl grows inside an oyster. When a tiny bit of something gets into its shell, the oyster covers it with layer after layer of shiny coating. |
| 5 | Frog songs | Many male frogs puff up a stretchy throat pouch when they call. The pouch helps make their song louder, so it carries farther. |
| 6 | Wind chimes | In a set of wind chimes, the longer tubes make lower sounds and the shorter tubes make higher sounds. |
| 9 | Silent owls | Owls can fly almost without a sound. The soft, fringed edges of their feathers quiet the rush of air. |
| 10 | Heavy clouds | A big puffy cloud can weigh about a million pounds. It floats because its water drops are tiny and spread out over a huge space. |
| 12 | Copycat singers | Mockingbirds can copy the songs of dozens of other birds. Some even copy sounds like squeaky gates and phone rings. |
| 13 | Learning to sing | Many young songbirds learn their songs by listening to grown-up birds, a little like how children learn to talk. |
| 15 | Two-note birds | Birds sing with a voice box deep in their chest called a syrinx. Some birds can make two different notes at the same time. |

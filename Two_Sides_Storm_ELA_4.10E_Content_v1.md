# Two Sides of the Storm — Content v1 (ELA.4.10E-XP)

**Grade 4 · ELAR · Cloudreach.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/two-sides-storm.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.10E · **Also shows up under:** 4.7B, 4.6F, 4.7C, 4.3B, 4.8B

**I can:** I can tell whether a story is told in first person or third person. I can explain how the point of view changes what the reader learns.

**Mission:** Read four tellings of the same storm, find the pronoun clues for each point of view, figure out what each narrator can and cannot know, and help the crew compare them.

**Story:** On the sky reef of Cloudreach, a windstorm slammed into Driftwing Reef. A young keeper tells what happened in her own words, a storyteller tells it from outside, and the news and her grandfather add their sides too.

**Meters on Cloudreach:** Tether strength (starts at 20%) · Keeper notes · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, Commander Vega here on Cloudreach. Last night a windstorm hit Driftwing Reef, the floating reef next to our outpost. A young keeper named Wren wrote down what happened. So did a storyteller on the reef. The two tellings sound very different. Help us figure out who is telling each one, and what each teller can and can't know. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The Night the Reef Hummed (Story told in first person, by Wren)

My name is Wren, and I am the youngest keeper on Driftwing Reef. `A1.1` Every morning, I check the cloud-coral and feed the glass-fin fish that swim through the mist. `A1.2` On the evening of the storm, I was alone at the east edge, coiling rope. `A1.3`

First, the air went still. `A2.1` Then the coral began to hum, low and deep, like a giant blowing across a bottle. `A2.2` I had never heard that sound before, and the hair on my arms stood straight up. `A2.3`

Dark clouds rolled in, and the wind slammed into the reef. `A3.1` The tether ropes that hold our reef to its sky anchors began to creak and groan. `A3.2` I grabbed the nearest one and braced my feet against a coral post. `A3.3` I wondered if Grandpa Bram was safe on the west side, but I could not see him through the rain. `A3.4`

For a long time, I just held on. `A4.1` My hands burned, and my heart thumped so hard that I could hear it over the wind. `A4.2` At last, the wind faded, and the coral stopped humming. `A4.3`

Then Grandpa came limping out of the mist. `A5.1` I ran to him and hugged him so hard that he laughed. `A5.2` "You held the east tether," he said. "I felt it hold." `A5.3`

### Passage B · Storm Over Driftwing Reef (Story told in third person, by a narrator)

On the evening of the storm, Wren was coiling rope at the east edge of Driftwing Reef. `B1.1` She was the youngest keeper on the reef, and she wanted to prove she was ready. `B1.2`

Across the reef, Grandpa Bram looked up from the kelp nets. `B2.1` He had heard the coral hum only once before, fifty years ago, and he knew what it meant. `B2.2` A big storm was coming, and his granddaughter was alone on the east side. `B2.3`

The wind hit the reef like a crashing wave. `B3.1` Wren grabbed a tether rope and dug in her heels. `B3.2` "I can hold it!" Wren shouted into the roaring wind. `B3.3` Her arms shook, but she did not let go. `B3.4`

On the west side, Bram tied the last kelp net to a post with a double knot. `B4.1` Then he started across the swaying rope bridge toward the east edge. `B4.2` Halfway across, he slipped and twisted his ankle, but he kept going. `B4.3`

Wren never saw her grandfather on the bridge. `B5.1` She only saw him limp out of the mist when the storm was over. `B5.2` "You held the east tether," Bram said. "I felt it hold." `B5.3` He was prouder of her than he could say, so he just hugged her tight. `B5.4`

### Passage C · Storm Pushes Driftwing Reef North (News report, from the Cloudreach Skyline Bulletin)

A powerful windstorm struck Driftwing Reef on Tuesday evening. `C1.1` The storm lasted about forty minutes. `C1.2`

Strong winds pushed the floating reef almost one mile north before its sky anchors caught. `C2.1` Two kelp nets were torn, and one rope bridge was damaged. `C2.2`

No one was seriously hurt. `C3.1` Keeper Bram Alder, age 68, twisted his ankle while crossing a bridge during the storm. `C3.2`

"Our youngest keeper held the east tether through the worst of it," Alder said. `C4.1` He added, "I am very proud of her." `C4.2`

Repairs to the reef will begin this week. `C5.1`

### Passage D · Nova's museum card (half rewritten) (Rewrite from first person to third person (has mistakes))

For a long time, Wren just held on. **My**`[D:My]` hands burned, and her heart thumped so hard that **I**`[D:I]` could hear it over the wind. At last, the wind faded, and the coral stopped humming around **me**`[D:me]` .

### Passage E · Grandpa Bram's Keeper Journal (Journal entry)

Tuesday night. `E1.1` My ankle is wrapped, and my granddaughter is asleep by the stove. `E1.2`

When the coral began to hum, my stomach dropped. `E2.1` The last time I heard that sound, I was a young keeper, and a storm tore our reef loose from two anchors. `E2.2`

I tied down the last kelp net and ran for the bridge. `E3.1` By the time I limped out of the mist, the storm was over. `E3.2`

Wren did not need me after all. `E4.1` Tomorrow, I will teach her the storm knots my own grandmother taught me. `E4.2`

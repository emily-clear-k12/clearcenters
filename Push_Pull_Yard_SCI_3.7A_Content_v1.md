# The Push and Pull Yard — Content v1 (SCI.3.7A-XP)

**Grade 3 · Science · Mechara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/push-pull-yard.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.7A · **Also shows up under:** 3.7B, 3.1A, 3.1B, 3.1E, 3.2B, 3.2C, 3.3A, 3.3C, 3.5B

**I can:** I can show and describe forces that act by touching, like pushes and pulls, and forces that act at a distance, like magnetism and gravity. I can test how pushes and pulls change the way things move.

**Mission:** Test pushes, pulls, magnets, and gravity so the new robots can graduate from the training yard.

**Story:** New robots are training in Mechara's robot yard. The crew tests pushes and pulls on carts, balls, and swings, and finds out what magnets and gravity can do without touching.

**Meters on Mechara:** Yard ready (starts at 20%) · Force flags · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

Before they can work in the city, they have to learn how pushes, pulls, magnets, and gravity move things. Forces here work just like they do on Earth. Test, measure, and explain. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Olympic tug-of-war | Tug-of-war used to be an Olympic sport. Teams pulled ropes at the Olympic Games from 1900 to 1920. |
| 3 | Frozen hockey pucks | Hockey pucks are kept frozen before a game. A frozen puck bounces less and slides more smoothly across the ice. |
| 5 | Seat belts | When a car stops suddenly, your body keeps moving forward. A seat belt pulls back on you so you stop safely with the car. |
| 6 | Floating trains | Maglev trains use strong magnets to float just above their tracks. With no wheels rubbing a track, some can go faster than 400 kilometers per hour. |
| 7 | Lodestone | Lodestone is a rock that is a natural magnet. It is made of an iron mineral called magnetite, and it can pick up small bits of iron. |
| 9 | Giant magnets in hospitals | Hospitals use MRI machines with huge magnets to take pictures of the inside of the body. The magnets are so strong that metal objects are not allowed in the room. |
| 10 | Curling stones | In the sport of curling, players slide a heavy stone across the ice. Teammates sweep the ice in front of it to help the stone slide farther and straighter. |
| 11 | Moon hops | Gravity on the Moon is only about one sixth as strong as on Earth. Astronauts there could bounce along in big, floating hops. |
| 12 | A hammer and a feather | In 1971, an astronaut on the Moon dropped a hammer and a feather at the same time. The Moon has no air to slow the feather, so they hit the ground together. |
| 15 | Swinging clocks | A swing is a kind of pendulum. Old grandfather clocks keep time with a pendulum that swings back and forth at a steady beat. |

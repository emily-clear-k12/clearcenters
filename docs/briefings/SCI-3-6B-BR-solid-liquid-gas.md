# Briefing: SCI-3-6B-BR — Solid, Liquid, or Gas?

**Subject:** Science · **Grade:** 3 · **TEKS:** 3.6B  
**TEKS text:** Describe and classify samples of matter as solids, liquids, and gases and demonstrate that solids have a definite shape and that liquids and gases take the shape of their container.  
**Minutes:** ~25 · **Modes:** Project or Solo (same four teach pages)  
**Feeds this activity:** Frequency Rush classify `3.FR.Matter-States` (Solid, Liquid, or Gas?)  
**World:** HQ Field Bench at Pebble Creek — painterly outdoor science table, jars, creek water, clipboard. Not Maple Crossing. Not chrome lab. Not photoreal. No animal-town. No close-up kid portraits.

This is the prove-the-mold write. Same spine as 3.2B. Different subject, different world, same cold-kid bar: a 3rd grader who has never heard 3.6B should finish this and then be able to sort a wooden block, honey, and air in a balloon in Frequency Rush.

**Title:** What is the same about these?

**S.A.M.:** Don’t name them yet. What is the same about these three things?

**Claim frame:** I think these are all __________.

**Chips:** wet · food · matter · the same shape

**Reveal:** Today is really about **what kind of matter** each one is. Keep your guess. HQ wants to know if it is a **solid**, a **liquid**, or a **gas**.

**Learning target:** Matter can be a **solid**, a **liquid**, or a **gas**. Solids **keep their own shape**. Liquids and gases **take the shape of their container**.

## Phase B — Field Brief (four pages)

Same pages for projection and solo. S.A.M. asks one question per page, waits for a chip, then prompts forward.

### Page 1 — Story
**Title:** Three things on the bench  
**Plate:** The same creek bench, morning light, HQ clipboard. Rock, water jar, air bag. No labels.

HQ set three things on the field bench by Pebble Creek: a rock, a jar of creek water, and a bag of air. They look different. They are all **matter**. HQ wants to know what kind of matter each one is.

Do **not** define solid / liquid / gas on this page.

**S.A.M.:** What does HQ want to know?  
Chips: What kind of matter each one is (`kind`) · Which one is the prettiest (`pretty`) · How heavy the rock is (`heavy`) → **kind**  
After: Right — what kind, not which one looks best.

### Page 2 — Idea
**Title:** Shape is the clue  
**Plate:** Three small panels on one spread: a block sitting in two different boxes (same block shape), water poured from a short cup into a tall jar (new shape), air squeezed from a bag into a bottle (fills the new bottle).

Matter can be a **solid**, a **liquid**, or a **gas**.

- A **solid** keeps its own shape. Move it to a new box. It still looks like itself.
- A **liquid** pours. It takes the shape of its container.
- A **gas** spreads out. It takes the shape of its container too — it fills the whole thing.

Soft things can still be solids. Sand can pour and still be lots of tiny solids.

**S.A.M.:** What is the clue that tells you the kind of matter?  
Chips: Color · Shape · How loud it is → **Shape**  
After: Shape. Does it keep its own, or take the container?

### Page 3 — Worked example
**Title:** HQ already tried the rock  
**Plate:** Same gray rock sitting in a shallow tray, then in a deep crate. Same rock shape both times.

HQ picked up the rock and set it in a new box. The rock did **not** change shape. It still looked like the rock. That is a **solid**. Solids keep their own shape.

HQ shows this finished classify. The student does not invent it.

**Compare pair:**
- First box: The rock looks like a rock.
- New box: The rock still looks like a rock.
- Same kind: solid.

**S.A.M.:** The rock went into a new box. Same shape or new shape?  
Chips: Same shape · New shape → **Same shape**  
After: Same shape. That’s how HQ knew it was a solid.

### Page 4 — Check
**Title:** Quick HQ check  
No extra checklist chrome. Two light questions, then practice.

**qc1:** HQ moved a wooden block into a new box. The block still looked like a block. Which kind?  
a Solid · b Liquid · c Gas → **a**

**qc2:** Creek water gets poured into a tall jar. What happens to its shape?  
a It keeps the same shape · b It takes the shape of the jar → **b**

**qc3:** Air fills up a balloon. What happens to its shape?  
a It keeps one solid shape · b It fills the whole balloon → **b**

## Phase C — Reason Sort (now you try)

**Title:** Bench Sort  
**Prompt:** Sort each sample. Use shape as the clue.

Three bins only (Grade 3). One obvious + one stretch per kind. Stretch items do **not** say the word solid / liquid / gas.

| id | text | bin | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| creek_rock | A gray rock sitting on the bench | solid | obvious |
| sand_jar | Dry sand that pours into a jar | solid | stretch — pours, still tiny solids |
| water_cup | Creek water poured into a cup | liquid | obvious |
| honey_drip | Honey dripping from a spoon | liquid | stretch — thick, still a liquid |
| balloon_air | Air filling up a balloon | gas | obvious |
| balloon_hiss | Air escaping with a hiss when you let go of a balloon | gas | stretch — fills the room, not the visible cloud |

After Sort, a cold kid should be able to tap **wooden block / honey / air in a balloon** in Frequency Rush without a new lesson.

## Phase D — Ops Choice (run two tests)

Not “pick 2 of 3 states.” The job is: which two **tests** prove the idea this period.

**Title:** Field Bench Tests  
**Constraint:** HQ’s clipboard only fits **two** tests today.  
**Scenario:** Pebble Creek Station has four cards. Three are real tests of shape. One is just for show.

**Cards:**
- `block_box` — Move a wooden block into a new box. (solid / keeps shape) TEKS
- `pour_jars` — Pour creek water into a short jar and a tall jar. (liquid / takes container) TEKS
- `bag_to_bottle` — Squeeze air from a bag into a bottle. (gas / fills container) TEKS
- `gold_stars` — Paint gold stars on the empty jars so the bench looks nice. (distractor)

Pick exactly 2. Name the real test that waits.

**Distractor fail:** Gold stars make the bench pretty. They do not tell you the kind of matter.

## Phase F — Clearance

No progress-gate checklist. Four questions, then HQ explains if they miss.

- **c1** What three kinds of matter did HQ study?  
  a solid, liquid, gas · b wet, dry, hot · c rock, wood, metal · d red, blue, green → **a**
- **c2** A wooden block goes in a new box and still looks like a block. Which kind? → **solid**
- **c3** True/False: If something is soft or pours, it must be a liquid. → **False** (sand is tiny solids; play-dough is a soft solid)
- **c4** Dynamic: the test still waiting, or the postcard scene — which kind fits?

**Trap line:** Soft or pourable is not the same as liquid.

**Subject:** Social Studies · **Grade:** 3 · **TEKS:** 3.1A  
**TEKS text:** Describe how individuals, events, and ideas have changed communities, past and present.  
**Also supports (light):** 3.15A vocabulary · 3.15C year / past-present (not the full skills strand)  
**Minutes:** ~25 · **Modes:** Project or Solo (same four teach pages)  
**Related Challenges:** none yet (`relatedChallengeIds: []`)  
**Sequel to:** SS-3-2A-BR Why Communities Form · SS-3-2B-BR How Communities Meet Needs (Maple Crossing already exists)  
**World:** Maple Crossing picture-book town — autumn light, creek, wooden bridge, little school. Same hand as 3.2A / 3.2B. Not Cloudreach-as-rival. Not photoreal. No animal-town. No close-up kid portraits.

This pack stays on **3.1A only**. Named people for 3.1B (L'Enfant, Banneker, Franklin) and 3.1C (Boone, Founding Fathers) get their own Briefings after this one teaches the three kinds of changers.

## Coverage map (the cold-kid test)

A Grade 3 kid with no social studies knowledge should finish this, then describe a new change as a person, an event, or an idea — including whether it is past or present.

| TEKS piece | Taught | Worked by HQ | Practiced in Sort | Challenge-ready? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual (one person) changes a community | Idea | Yes — Cal builds the creek bridge | Carpenter builds a town hall (obvious) + a nurse opens a clinic (stretch) | Yes |
| Event changes a community | Idea + Check qc2 | Named, not fully worked | Creek flood (obvious) + harvest festival that starts a market (stretch) | Yes |
| Idea changes a community | Idea + Check qc3 | Named, not fully worked | "Kids should have a school" (obvious) + "keep the creek clean" (stretch) | Yes |
| Past vs present | Story + Idea | Bridge then / now | — (Clearance c3) | Light |

Field Brief works **one** change all the way (an individual). Sort is “now you try” for event and idea, plus a stretch individual so a later Challenge item doesn’t feel new.

## Phase A — Intel Drop (mystery open)

**Plate:** Split picture of the **same** creek bend, unlabeled. Left: dirt path, no bridge, no school, a few houses. Right: wooden bridge, little school, more rooftops. Do **not** put the words past / present / person / event / idea on the art.

**Title:** What changed here?

**S.A.M.:** Don’t name it yet. What changed in this town?

**Claim frame:** I think this town changed because of __________.

**Chips:** the weather · a person · a party · the town grew

**Reveal:** Today is really about **how communities change**. Keep your guess. HQ wants to know if a change came from a **person**, an **event**, or an **idea**.

**Learning target:** Communities change. An **individual** is one person. An **event** is something that happens. An **idea** is a new thought people try. **Past** is how the town used to be. **Present** is how it is now.

---

## Phase B — Field Brief (four pages)

Same pages for projection and solo. S.A.M. asks one question per page, waits for a chip, then prompts forward.

### Page 1 — Story
**Title:** The old picture  
**Plate:** HQ clipboard on a porch rail, holding the old dirt-path picture, with today’s Maple Crossing (bridge + school) behind it.

HQ found an old picture of Maple Crossing. The creek is the same. The path is dirt. There is no bridge. There is no school. HQ is here to learn **what changed the town**.

Do **not** define individual / event / idea on this page.

**S.A.M.:** What is HQ coming to learn?  
Chips: What changed the town (`changed`) · Which town is prettier (`pretty`) · What the creek is named (`name`) → **changed**  
After: Right — what changed, not which picture looks nicer.

### Page 2 — Idea
**Title:** Three kinds of changers  
**Plate:** One spread, three small panels: a carpenter at a new town hall (person), floodwater over a dirt path (event), a porch meeting with a “kids should have a school” notice (idea). No photo-real faces; mid-distance storybook figures.

Communities change. Three kinds of changers:

- An **individual** is one person who does something that changes the town.
- An **event** is something that happens — a flood, a fair, a new train.
- An **idea** is a new thought people try.

**Past** means how the town used to be. **Present** means how it is now.

**S.A.M.:** What are the three kinds of changers?  
Chips: Person, event, idea (`three`) · Big, bigger, biggest (`size`) · Red, gold, blue (`color`) → **three**  
After: Person, event, idea. HQ will show you one.

### Page 3 — Worked example
**Title:** HQ already looked at the bridge  
**Plate:** Same creek bend. Left: dirt path ending at the water. Right: Cal’s wooden bridge in place, wagon crossing. Same trees, same houses. Not a kid close-up.

HQ looked at one change all the way. A builder named **Cal** cut wood and made a bridge where the dirt path met the creek. People can cross now. That is an **individual**. One person changed the town.

The student does not invent this. HQ shows it finished.

**Compare pair:**
- Past: Dirt path. You had to stop at the water.
- Present: A wooden bridge. People and wagons can cross.
- Kind of changer: individual.

**S.A.M.:** Cal built the bridge. What kind of changer is Cal?  
Chips: A person (`person`) · An event (`event`) · An idea (`idea`) → **person**  
After: A person. That’s how HQ knew the bridge was an individual change.

### Page 4 — Check
**Title:** Quick HQ check  
No extra checklist chrome. Three light questions (one per kind), then practice.

**qc1:** Cal built a bridge so people could cross the creek. Which kind of changer?  
a A person · b An event · c An idea → **a**

**qc2:** A flood washed out the old dirt path. People built a higher road. Which kind of changer is the flood?  
a A person · b An event → **b**

**qc3:** The town said, “Kids should have a school,” and then they made one. Which kind of changer is that thought?  
a An event · b An idea → **b**

## Phase C — Reason Sort (now you try)

**Title:** Change Sort  
**Prompt:** Sort each change. Is it a person, an event, or an idea?

Three bins only (Grade 3). One obvious + one stretch per kind. Stretch items do **not** say the words individual / event / idea.

| id | text | bin | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| town_hall | A carpenter builds a new town hall | individual | obvious |
| clinic | A nurse opens a little clinic on Main Street | individual | stretch — job, still one person |
| creek_flood | A flood covers the dirt path | event | obvious |
| harvest_fair | A harvest festival that brings a Saturday market | event | stretch — happy event, still an event |
| school_idea | The town says kids should have a school | idea | obvious |
| clean_creek | The town says we should keep the creek clean | idea | stretch — not a building, still an idea |

After Sort, a cold kid should be able to look at a new card (a mayor, a storm, a new rule) and name the kind without a new lesson.

## Phase D — Ops Choice (study two changes)

Not “pick 2 of 3 vocab words.” The job is: which two **changes** HQ should study this week.

**Title:** Maple Crossing Changes  
**Constraint:** HQ’s clipboard only fits **two** changes today.  
**Scenario:** Maple Crossing Station has four cards. Three are real town changes. One is just for show. HQ wrote: We can only study two changes today. Which two should we study so we learn about a person, an event, and an idea?

**Cards:**
- `cal_bridge` — Cal builds the creek bridge. (individual) TEKS
- `flood_road` — A flood washes out the path; people build a higher road. (event) TEKS
- `school_thought` — The town tries the idea that kids should have a school. (idea) TEKS
- `gold_frame` — Paint a gold frame on the old picture so it looks fancy. (distractor)

Pick exactly 2. Name the real change that waits.

**Distractor fail:** A gold frame makes the old picture pretty. It does not change the town.

## Phase E — Evidence Drop (postcard)

**Front:** Pick a scene that clearly shows **one** kind of changer.  
Scenes: Cal’s bridge · flood then higher road · porch notice for a school

**Back frame:** This town changed because of a __________. I know because __________.

- reasonOptions: person · event · idea  
- evidence chips: one person did the work · something happened to the town · people tried a new thought

---

## Phase F — Clearance

No progress-gate checklist. Four questions, then HQ explains if they miss.

- **c1** What three kinds of changers did HQ study?  
  a person, event, idea · b food, clothes, toys · c big, small, medium · d red, gold, blue → **a**
- **c2** Cal built a bridge. Which kind of changer? → **person / individual**
- **c3** True/False: Past means how the town is right now. → **False** (past = how it used to be; present = how it is now)
- **c4** Dynamic: the change still waiting, or the postcard scene — which kind fits?

**Trap line:** Pretty pictures are not a town change. A person, an event, or an idea is.

**Cleared:** Briefing cleared: How Towns Change. Ask your teacher when you’re ready for a Challenge.

## What this is proving

The 3.2B mold survives a History skill that is not “compare two towns”:

- Four teach pages, not one quiz per category
- All three TEKS kinds get practiced (3 bins)
- Worked page does one change fully (individual / the bridge)
- Sort does event + idea, plus a stretch person
- Ops is a decision about **which changes to study**, not a copy of “pick two needs”
- Past / present is taught in Idea and checked in Clearance, not as a fourth sort bin
- Next History Briefing can be **SS-3-1B-BR** (L'Enfant, Banneker, Franklin) using the same mold

If a cold kid can finish this and then name a new change, the History strand is ready to keep moving. If a page feels thin, we fix it here before 3.1B.

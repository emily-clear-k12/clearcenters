# The Garbled Messages — Content v1 (ELA.3.11D-XP)

**Grade 3 · ELAR · Cindara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/garbled-messages.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.11D · **Also shows up under:** 3.11C

**I can:** I can edit a draft to fix grammar, capital letters, punctuation, and spelling. I can revise sentences so they are clear.

**Mission:** Find and fix the mistakes in the crew's messages, logs, and letters, then send a clear report to Mission Control.

**Story:** A lava storm on fiery Cindara scrambled the outpost's radio messages. Every message needs fixing before it can be sent.

**Meters on Cindara:** Signal (starts at 15%) · Messages · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega on Cindara. Can you hear me? The signal is weak. A lava storm swept over our outpost last night. The hot ash scrambled every radio message we tried to send. Mission Control can't read garbled messages. Help us fix each one before it goes out. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Ring of Fire | Most of Earth's active volcanoes sit in a giant horseshoe shape around the Pacific Ocean. It is called the Ring of Fire. |
| 2 | Pillow lava | When lava flows into the ocean, the cold water cools it fast. It hardens into round lumps that look like pillows, called pillow lava. |
| 4 | Volcanic lightning | Lightning can flash inside the dark cloud above an erupting volcano. Bits of rock and ash rub together and build up electricity. |
| 5 | A volcano in a cornfield | In 1943, a new volcano started growing in a farmer's cornfield in Mexico. It was named Parícutin, and in its first year it grew more than 300 meters (about 1,000 feet) tall. |
| 7 | Lava tubes | Sometimes the top of a lava flow cools into a hard crust while hot lava keeps flowing underneath. When the lava drains out, it leaves a tunnel called a lava tube. |
| 8 | Smooth and rough lava | Some lava cools into smooth, ropy swirls called pahoehoe. Other lava cools into sharp, chunky rocks called a'a. Both names are Hawaiian words. |
| 10 | Where "volcano" comes from | The word volcano comes from Vulcan, the name of the Roman god of fire. |
| 11 | Volcanoes under the sea | Most of Earth's volcanoes are hidden under the ocean. Many of them erupt deep underwater where no one can see. |
| 13 | The biggest volcano | The biggest known volcano in our solar system is Olympus Mons on Mars. It is more than twice as tall as Mount Everest. |
| 15 | Active, dormant, extinct | Scientists sort volcanoes into three groups. Active volcanoes have erupted recently. Dormant ones are sleeping but could wake up. Extinct ones are not expected to erupt ever again. |

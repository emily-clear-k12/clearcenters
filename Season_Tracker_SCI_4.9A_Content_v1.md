# The Season Tracker — Content v1 (SCI.4.9A-XP)

**Grade 4 · Science · Frostveil.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/season-tracker.js`.

**Main standard:** 4.9A · **Also shows up under:** 4.1E, 4.1F, 4.2B, 4.2C, 4.3A, 4.3C, 4.5A, 4.5B

**I can:** I can collect and study data about daylight and temperature. I can use patterns to put the seasons in order and predict what will change next.

**Mission:** Study real daylight and temperature data from Earth, find the pattern in the seasons, and switch on a tracker that can predict what comes next.

**Story:** The Frostveil crew is building a Season Tracker for planet Earth. Weather stations in Texas, Alaska, and Australia send data, and the crew finds the pattern.

**Meters on Frostveil:** Tracker calibrated (starts at 25%) · Data packets · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

Our job is to build a Season Tracker that finds the pattern and predicts what comes next. Stay warm, and watch the numbers. Vega out.

## Discoveries (Earth-true)

| Task | Discovery | Fact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | The longest day | In the northern half of Earth, the longest day of the year comes around June 21. It is called the summer solstice. |
| 4 | Weather averages | Weather scientists find a month's average temperature by using 30 years of daily readings. That's why the numbers are called 'about.' |
| 5 | Equal day and night | Around March 20 and September 22, day and night are about 12 hours each almost everywhere on Earth. These days are called equinoxes. |
| 6 | Closest in January | Earth is closest to the Sun in early January. That's winter in Texas! Earth's distance from the Sun changes only a little, so it doesn't cause the seasons. |
| 7 | The midnight sun | In Utqiagvik, the northernmost town in Alaska, the Sun doesn't set at all for more than 80 days in summer. People call it the midnight sun. |
| 8 | Steady days | Near the equator, daylight stays close to 12 hours all year long. The farther a place is from the equator, the more its daylight changes. |
| 9 | Hottest after longest | The longest day comes in June, but Texas is usually hottest in July and August. Land and water take weeks to warm up, like a pool that stays chilly after the first hot day. |
| 10 | Opposite seasons | When it's winter in the northern half of Earth, it's summer in the southern half. Many people in Australia go to the beach on the holidays in December. |
| 12 | Direct sunlight | When the Sun is high in the sky, its light hits the ground more directly and heats it more. That's one big reason summer is warmer than winter. |
| 15 | Earth's tilt | Earth's axis is tilted about 23 and a half degrees. As Earth goes around the Sun, the axis keeps leaning the same way, so each half takes turns getting more sunlight. |

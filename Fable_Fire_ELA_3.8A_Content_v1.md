# The Fable Fire — Content v1 (ELA.3.8A-XP)

**Grade 3 · ELAR · Cindara.** Written Sept 28, 2026. Code: `lib/cases/expedition-station/quests/fable-fire.js`.

**Main standard:** 3.8A · **Also shows up under:** 3.9A, 3.6F, 3.7C, 3.3B, 3.8B, 3.8C, 3.7B

**I can:** I can figure out the theme of a story and tell it apart from the topic. I can tell fables, folktales, and fairy tales apart.

**Mission:** Listen to the tales of Cindara, find each one's theme, and prove it with evidence before the collection goes to Mission Control.

**Story:** Around a lava-rock fire on Cindara, the villagers tell their oldest fables and folktales. The crew wants to collect them all, but each tale's lesson is hidden inside the story.

**Meters on Cindara:** Story fire (starts at 25%) · Story stones · Journal (10 discoveries)

**Status:** All 3 acts are playable. Every task is graded (written answers are scored by the teacher).

**Opening (Vega):** Specialist {firstName}, this is Commander Vega on Cindara. Tonight we are guests at the Ember Ring. The villagers gather around a lava-rock fire to tell their oldest fables and folktales. Fire-keeper Sarn has agreed to let us collect them. Every tale hides a lesson. Help us find each theme, and prove it. Vega out.

## Passages

### Passage A · The Cinder Fox and the Last Ember (Fable)

On the coldest night of the year, the great fire on Cindara burned low. `A1.1` Only one bright ember was left, glowing orange in the ashes. `A1.2` Cinder Fox saw it first. `A1.3` "Mine!" she said, and she tucked the ember under her thick, bushy tail. `A1.4`

Soon a lava lizard crept up, shivering. `A2.1` "Please, Fox, may I warm my toes by your ember?" he asked. `A2.2` "No," said Fox. "If I share it, there will be less heat for me." `A2.3` A family of glow moths asked next, and Fox turned her back on them too. `A2.4`

But an ember needs air to keep burning. `A3.1` Under Fox's heavy tail, the ember was smothered. `A3.2` Its orange glow faded to gray, and then it went cold. `A3.3` Fox shivered alone in the dark. `A3.4`

Across the rocks, she saw a warm light. `A4.1` The lizard and the moths had piled their tiny sparks and dry moss in a ring. `A4.2` Together, they had made a crackling fire. `A4.3` "Come and sit with us," called the lizard. "There is room for everyone." `A4.4` Fox sat down, and her cheeks grew hot, but not from the fire. `A4.5`

Moral: A fire that is shared warms everyone, but a fire kept to yourself can leave you cold. `A5.1`

### Passage B · Why the Pebble Crabs Guard the Lava River (Folktale)

Long, long ago, before anyone can remember, a village sat beside the Red River of Cindara. `B1.1` The river was not made of water. It was made of slow, glowing lava. `B1.2` One day, the lava began to rise and creep toward the village. `B1.3`

The villagers ran to the strongest creature they knew, the great Rock Bull. `B2.1` "Please push the lava back!" they begged. `B2.2` Rock Bull pushed with all his might, but the lava was too hot, and he backed away. `B2.3`

Next the villagers asked Flame Hawk, the fastest flier in the sky. `B3.1` Flame Hawk flapped her wings to cool the lava, but it only glowed brighter. `B3.2` The lava kept creeping closer. `B3.3`

At last, a tiny pebble crab stepped forward. `B4.1` "We can help," she said. `B4.2` Rock Bull laughed. "You are no bigger than a seed!" `B4.3`

The crab did not argue. `B5.1` She picked up one cool, gray stone and set it at the edge of the river. `B5.2` Then a hundred more pebble crabs came, and then a thousand. `B5.3` Each crab carried just one stone, but stone by stone, they built a long, low wall. `B5.4` The lava bumped against the wall and stopped. `B5.5`

The village was saved, and Rock Bull bowed his great head to the tiny crabs. `B6.1` That is why, even today, pebble crabs line the banks of the Red River, each one guarding a single stone. `B6.2` And that is why the people of Cindara still say, "No helper is too small." `B6.3`

### Passage C · The Smallest Spark (Fable)

Spark Beetle had the smallest light on all of Cindara. `C1.1` Her glow was only as big as a grain of sand. `C1.2` The lantern lizards, whose tails glowed like torches, laughed at her. "Why do you even bother to shine?" they said. `C1.3` Spark Beetle felt so small that she hid under a rock. `C1.4`

One night, a great cloud of ash rolled over the hills. `C2.1` The lantern lizards ran into their caves and hid their tails from the ash. `C2.2` In the dark, a young moth named Dusk lost her way home. `C2.3` "Help!" she cried. "I can't see anything!" `C2.4`

Spark Beetle heard her. `C3.1` She was scared, but she crawled out and let her tiny light glow. `C3.2` "Follow me," she called. `C3.3` Dusk followed the little dot of light, step by step, all the way home. `C3.4`

The next morning, the lantern lizards heard what had happened. `C4.1` They never laughed at Spark Beetle again. `C4.2`

Moral: No light is too small to help someone find the way. `C5.1`

### Passage D · The Singing Stone (Fairy tale)

Once upon a time, a young prince lived in a castle of black glass on top of a volcano. `D1.1` In the castle garden sat a magic stone that sang whenever someone told the truth. `D1.2`

One day, the prince broke his mother's favorite bowl. `D2.1` He hid the pieces and said, "The wind knocked it over." `D2.2` The magic stone stayed silent. `D2.3`

All week, the prince could not sleep. `D3.1` His secret felt as heavy as a mountain. `D3.2`

At last, he went to the queen. `D4.1` "I broke your bowl, and I am sorry," he said. `D4.2` At once, the magic stone began to sing, and the prince felt as light as a feather. `D4.3` The queen hugged him. "A bowl can be fixed," she said. "Trust is much harder to fix." `D4.4`
